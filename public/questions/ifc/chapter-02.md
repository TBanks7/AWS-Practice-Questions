# Chapter 2: Overview of the Canadian Financial Marketplace

Original practice questions grounded in the CSI Investment Funds in Canada (IFC) textbook, Chapter 2: investment capital, financial instruments, financial markets, financial intermediaries, and the Canadian securities regulatory framework.

---

1. According to the textbook, capital is best described as having which set of characteristics?
    - A. Fixed, regulated, and abundant.
    - B. Illiquid, domestic, and government-controlled.
    - C. Mobile, sensitive to its environment, and scarce.
    - D. Guaranteed, inflation-proof, and unlimited.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
    </details>

2. Which of the following is generally NOT considered an important supplier of new capital to others in the capital markets?
    - A. Non-financial corporations, since most of their retained earnings are used internally rather than invested in others' stocks and bonds.
    - B. Retail investors.
    - C. Institutional investors.
    - D. Foreign investors.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
    </details>

3. A pension fund that trades large volumes of securities and provides a steady flow of money to invest is best classified as:
    - A. A retail investor.
    - B. A foreign investor.
    - C. A non-deposit-taking bank.
    - D. An institutional investor.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
    </details>

4. When the federal government needs to borrow to cover a shortfall between revenue and expenditures, it primarily issues:
    - A. Instalment (serial) debentures.
    - B. Treasury bills and marketable bonds.
    - C. Common shares.
    - D. Mutual fund units.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
    </details>

5. Municipalities often finance long-lived infrastructure, such as sewers and waterworks expected to last twenty years or more, by issuing:
    - A. Preferred shares.
    - B. Short-term commercial paper only.
    - C. Instalment or serial debentures, spreading the cost over the asset's useful life.
    - D. Foreign currency bonds exclusively.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
    </details>

6. A client is surprised to learn that a company can skip a dividend payment on its common shares without violating any contract, while missing a bond interest payment would put the company in default. This difference exists because:
    - A. Dividends are discretionary distributions of profit, while bond interest is a contractual, obligatory payment.
    - B. Bonds and shares are legally identical instruments.
    - C. Dividends are guaranteed by the government.
    - D. Bond interest is optional in Canada.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
    </details>

7. The most common form of investment fund, which raises capital by selling shares or units to investors and is redeemable on demand at the current value of the fund's portfolio, is known as a(n):
    - A. Closed-end fund.
    - B. Derivative.
    - C. Debenture.
    - D. Open-end fund, also known as a mutual fund.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
    </details>

8. Options and forwards, which derive their value from an underlying stock or index and are generally suited to more sophisticated investors, are examples of:
    - A. Equity instruments.
    - B. Derivatives.
    - C. Debt instruments.
    - D. Deposit-taking products.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
    </details>

9. When a company sells its shares to the public for the very first time, this transaction takes place in the primary market and is known as a(n):
    - A. Initial public offering (IPO).
    - B. Secondary distribution.
    - C. Dealer market transaction.
    - D. Rights offering only.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
    </details>

10. An investor sells shares they already own to another investor through the stock exchange. Which statement about this transaction is correct?
    - A. The issuing company receives the sale proceeds.
    - B. This transaction takes place in the primary market.
    - C. The issuing company receives none of the proceeds, since it was already paid when the securities were first issued.
    - D. Mutual funds cannot be bought or sold this way.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
    </details>

11. In an auction market, the highest price a buyer is willing to pay for a stock is called the ______, and the lowest price a seller will accept is called the ______.
    - A. Ask price; bid price.
    - B. Spread; premium.
    - C. Opening price; closing price.
    - D. Bid price; ask price.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
    </details>

12. An investor asks which Canadian exchange focuses on trading junior, early-stage securities rather than senior blue-chip equities. The representative should identify:
    - A. The Toronto Stock Exchange (TSX).
    - B. The TSX Venture Exchange.
    - C. The Montreal Exchange.
    - D. ICE NGX Canada.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
    </details>

13. A client wants to trade financial and equity futures and options contracts. Which Canadian exchange specializes in this activity?
    - A. The Montreal Exchange (Bourse de Montréal).
    - B. The Canadian Securities Exchange (CSE).
    - C. The NEO Exchange.
    - D. The TSX Venture Exchange.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
    </details>

14. Which of the following would you expect to observe in a highly liquid market?
    - A. Infrequent trades and a wide bid-ask spread.
    - B. Large, unpredictable price swings between trades.
    - C. A market with almost no trading volume.
    - D. Frequent sales, a narrow bid-ask spread, and small price fluctuations between trades.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
    </details>

15. Almost all bonds and debentures in Canada trade on:
    - A. The Toronto Stock Exchange auction market.
    - B. The TSX Venture Exchange.
    - C. Dealer (over-the-counter) markets, negotiated directly between dealers.
    - D. A physical trading floor open to retail investors.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
    </details>

16. Financial intermediaries are generally divided into two broad categories:
    - A. Federal and provincial institutions.
    - B. Deposit-taking institutions (such as banks) and non-deposit-taking institutions (such as insurance companies).
    - C. Domestic and foreign institutions only.
    - D. Auction-market and dealer-market institutions.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
    </details>

17. Investment dealers play a significant role in the securities industry primarily by performing which TWO functions? Select the option that correctly names BOTH.
    - A. Underwriting and distributing new securities in the primary market, and maintaining secondary market liquidity.
    - B. Setting the Bank of Canada's overnight rate and printing currency.
    - C. Regulating mutual fund dealers and insurance companies.
    - D. Accepting retail deposits and issuing mortgages.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
    </details>

18. Chartered banks in Canada operate under legislation that is regularly revised, typically every five years, called the:
    - A. Canada Business Corporations Act.
    - B. Criminal Code.
    - C. Consumer Protection Act.
    - D. Bank Act.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
    </details>

19. Credit unions and caisses populaires originally emerged in the early 1900s largely because:
    - A. Federal legislation required every community to have one.
    - B. Chartered banks were prohibited from operating in rural areas.
    - C. Many savers and borrowers felt chartered banks were too profit-oriented.
    - D. They were created to compete directly with life insurance companies.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
    </details>

20. A new representative assumes that mutual fund sales are regulated primarily at the federal level, similar to banking. Is this accurate?
    - A. Yes, all securities regulation in Canada is federal.
    - B. No, most legislation on the trading and distribution of securities is a provincial matter, coordinated nationally through the Canadian Securities Administrators.
    - C. Yes, but only for mutual funds specifically.
    - D. No, securities regulation is entirely municipal.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
    </details>

21. If a self-regulatory organization's rule is stricter than the corresponding provincial securities rule, which rule applies?
    - A. The provincial rule always overrides the SRO rule.
    - B. Neither rule applies until the conflict is resolved by the CSA.
    - C. The representative may choose whichever rule is more convenient.
    - D. The more stringent of the two rules applies.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
    </details>

22. The Mutual Fund Dealers Association (MFDA) is responsible for regulating:
    - A. The distribution side of the mutual fund industry — that is, mutual fund dealers — but not the mutual funds themselves.
    - B. The investment policies and holdings of individual mutual funds.
    - C. All investment dealers trading any type of security in Canada.
    - D. Chartered banks and trust companies.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
    </details>

23. In Quebec, which TWO bodies share responsibility for regulating the mutual fund industry? (Choose TWO)
    - A. The Investment Industry Regulatory Organization of Canada (IIROC).
    - B. The Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF), which oversees fund company operations.
    - C. The Mutual Fund Dealers Association (MFDA) acting alone, with no provincial involvement.
    - D. The Bank of Canada.
    - E. The Chambre de la sécurité financière, which sets continuing education requirements and enforces a code of ethics.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B, E
    </details>

24. Which statement about the Investment Industry Regulatory Organization of Canada (IIROC) is correct?
    - A. Membership is optional for Canadian investment dealers.
    - B. IIROC regulates mutual fund dealers exclusively.
    - C. All Canadian investment dealers must be members of IIROC, which sets rules on proficiency, conduct, and market integrity.
    - D. IIROC only regulates trading on the TSX Venture Exchange.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
    </details>

25. Unlike common shares, which trade in both primary and secondary markets, mutual fund units are:
    - A. Only ever traded in the secondary market between investors.
