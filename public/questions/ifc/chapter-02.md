# Chapter 2: Overview of the Canadian Financial Marketplace

Original practice questions grounded in the CSI Investment Funds in Canada (IFC) textbook, Chapter 2: investment capital, financial instruments, financial markets, financial intermediaries, and the Canadian securities regulatory framework.

---

1. According to the textbook, capital is best described as being:
    - A. Stable, abundant, and domestically controlled.
    - B. Mobile, abundant, and insensitive to risk.
    - C. Mobile, sensitive to its environment, and scarce.
    - D. Immobile, scarce, and insensitive to its environment.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Capital moves easily across borders (mobile), flows toward jurisdictions with favourable political and economic conditions (sensitive to its environment), and is limited in supply (scarce).
    </details>

2. Which of the following is generally NOT considered an important supplier of new capital to others in the capital markets?
    - A. Non-financial corporations.
    - B. Retail investors.
    - C. Institutional investors.
    - D. Foreign investors.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: Non-financial corporations mostly reinvest their retained earnings in their own operations instead of buying other issuers' stocks and bonds. Retail, institutional, and foreign investors are the main suppliers of capital.
    </details>

3. A pension fund that trades large volumes of securities and provides a steady flow of money to invest is best classified as:
    - A. A retail investor.
    - B. A foreign investor.
    - C. A deposit-taking institution.
    - D. An institutional investor.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: Pension funds, insurance companies, and similar organizations that invest large pools of money on behalf of others are institutional investors.
    </details>

4. When the federal government needs to borrow to cover a shortfall between revenue and expenditures, it primarily issues:
    - A. Instalment (serial) debentures.
    - B. Treasury bills and marketable bonds.
    - C. Guaranteed investment certificates.
    - D. Bankers' acceptances.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: The federal government finances deficits with Treasury bills (short-term) and marketable bonds. Serial debentures are a municipal tool, and GICs and bankers' acceptances are issued by financial institutions and corporations.
    </details>

5. Municipalities often finance long-lived infrastructure, such as sewers and waterworks, by issuing:
    - A. Preferred shares.
    - B. Short-term commercial paper.
    - C. Instalment or serial debentures.
    - D. Treasury bills.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Serial debentures mature in instalments, so repayment is spread over the useful life of the asset being financed. Municipalities don't issue shares, and commercial paper and T-bills are short-term.
    </details>

6. A client is surprised that a company can skip a common share dividend without breaching any contract, while missing a bond interest payment would put it in default. This is because:
    - A. Dividends are discretionary, while bond interest is a contractual obligation.
    - B. Dividends are guaranteed by the issuer, while bond interest is not.
    - C. Bond interest is paid only after dividends have been paid.
    - D. Dividends are paid from capital, while interest is paid from profits.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: Bondholders are creditors with a contractual right to interest. Shareholders are owners, and dividends are paid only at the board's discretion.
    </details>

7. The most common form of investment fund, which raises capital by selling units to investors and redeems them on demand at the current value of the portfolio, is a(n):
    - A. Closed-end fund.
    - B. Segregated fund.
    - C. Exchange-traded fund.
    - D. Open-end fund.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: An open-end fund (a mutual fund) continuously issues and redeems units at net asset value. A closed-end fund has a fixed number of shares that trade on an exchange.
    </details>

8. Options and forwards, which derive their value from an underlying stock or index and are generally suited to more sophisticated investors, are examples of:
    - A. Equity instruments.
    - B. Derivatives.
    - C. Debt instruments.
    - D. Investment funds.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: Derivatives get their value from an underlying asset such as a stock, index, or commodity. Options and forwards are the textbook's examples.
    </details>

9. When a company sells its shares to the public for the very first time, the transaction is known as a(n):
    - A. Initial public offering.
    - B. Secondary offering.
    - C. Private placement.
    - D. Rights offering.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: A company's first sale of shares to the public is an IPO, and it takes place in the primary market.
    </details>

10. An investor sells shares they already own to another investor through the stock exchange. Which statement about this transaction is correct?
    - A. The issuing company receives the sale proceeds.
    - B. The transaction takes place in the primary market.
    - C. The issuing company receives none of the proceeds.
    - D. The issuing company receives a small commission.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: This is a secondary market trade between investors. The issuer received its money when the shares were first sold in the primary market and gets nothing from later trades.
    </details>

11. In an auction market, the highest price a buyer is willing to pay is called the ______, and the lowest price a seller will accept is called the ______.
    - A. Ask price; bid price.
    - B. Spread; premium.
    - C. Opening price; closing price.
    - D. Bid price; ask price.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: The bid is the highest price a buyer will pay and the ask is the lowest price a seller will accept. The difference between them is the spread.
    </details>

12. An investor asks which Canadian exchange focuses on junior, early-stage securities rather than senior equities. The representative should identify:
    - A. The Toronto Stock Exchange.
    - B. The TSX Venture Exchange.
    - C. The Montreal Exchange.
    - D. ICE NGX Canada.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: The TSX Venture Exchange lists junior securities. The TSX handles senior equities, the Montreal Exchange handles derivatives, and ICE NGX handles natural gas and electricity.
    </details>

13. A client wants to trade financial and equity futures and options contracts. Which Canadian exchange specializes in this activity?
    - A. The Montreal Exchange.
    - B. The Canadian Securities Exchange.
    - C. The NEO Exchange.
    - D. The TSX Venture Exchange.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: The Montreal Exchange (Bourse de Montréal) is Canada's exchange for financial and equity futures and options.
    </details>

14. Which of the following would you expect to observe in a highly liquid market?
    - A. Infrequent trades and a wide bid-ask spread.
    - B. Frequent trades and a wide bid-ask spread.
    - C. Infrequent trades and small price changes between trades.
    - D. Frequent trades, a narrow spread, and small price changes.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: Liquidity means frequent trading, a narrow bid-ask spread, and small price changes from one trade to the next. All three have to be present.
    </details>

15. Almost all bonds and debentures in Canada trade on:
    - A. The TSX auction market.
    - B. The TSX Venture Exchange.
    - C. Dealer (over-the-counter) markets.
    - D. The Montreal Exchange.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Bonds and debentures trade in dealer markets, where dealers negotiate trades with each other and with clients. By dollar volume, this market is larger than the equity market.
    </details>

16. Financial intermediaries are generally divided into two broad categories:
    - A. Federal and provincial institutions.
    - B. Deposit-taking and non-deposit-taking institutions.
    - C. Domestic and foreign institutions.
    - D. Auction-market and dealer-market institutions.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: Banks, trust companies, and credit unions take deposits. Insurance companies, pension funds, and investment dealers are intermediaries that don't.
    </details>

17. Investment dealers play a significant role in the securities industry by performing which TWO functions?
    - A. Underwriting new issues and providing secondary market liquidity.
    - B. Setting benchmark interest rates and insuring client deposits.
    - C. Regulating mutual fund dealers and licensing insurance agents.
    - D. Accepting retail deposits and issuing residential mortgages.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: Investment dealers underwrite and distribute new securities in the primary market and support liquidity by trading in the secondary market.
    </details>

18. Chartered banks in Canada operate under legislation that is regularly revised, typically every five years, called the:
    - A. Canada Business Corporations Act.
    - B. Criminal Code.
    - C. Consumer Protection Act.
    - D. Bank Act.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: Chartered banks are governed by the federal Bank Act, which is reviewed and revised about every five years.
    </details>

19. Credit unions and caisses populaires originally emerged in the early 1900s largely because:
    - A. Federal legislation required every community to have one.
    - B. Chartered banks were barred from operating in rural areas.
    - C. Many people felt the chartered banks were too profit-oriented.
    - D. Life insurers wanted a deposit-taking affiliate.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: These member-owned co-operatives grew out of a feeling among many savers and borrowers that the chartered banks were too focused on profit.
    </details>

20. A new representative assumes that mutual fund sales are regulated mainly at the federal level, like banking. Is this accurate?
    - A. Yes; securities are regulated federally, like banks.
    - B. No; securities regulation is mainly provincial, coordinated by the CSA.
    - C. Yes; mutual funds alone are regulated federally.
    - D. No; securities are regulated mainly by municipalities.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: Trading and distribution of securities is mainly a provincial matter. Each province has its own securities act and regulator, and the Canadian Securities Administrators coordinate them nationally.
    </details>

21. If a self-regulatory organization's rule is stricter than the corresponding provincial securities rule, which rule applies?
    - A. The provincial rule always applies.
    - B. The SRO rule always applies.
    - C. The representative may choose either rule.
    - D. The more stringent of the two rules.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: When an SRO rule and a provincial rule differ, the more stringent one applies. That isn't always the SRO rule; it depends on which is stricter.
    </details>

22. The Mutual Fund Dealers Association (MFDA) is responsible for regulating:
    - A. Mutual fund dealers, but not the mutual funds themselves.
    - B. The investment policies of individual mutual funds.
    - C. All investment dealers trading any type of security.
    - D. Chartered banks and trust companies.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: The MFDA regulates the distribution side — mutual fund dealers and their representatives. The funds themselves are regulated by the provincial securities commissions, and investment dealers fall under IIROC.
    </details>

23. In Quebec, which TWO bodies share responsibility for regulating the mutual fund industry? (Choose TWO)
    - A. The Investment Industry Regulatory Organization of Canada (IIROC).
    - B. The Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF).
    - C. The Mutual Fund Dealers Association, acting alone.
    - D. The Bank of Canada.
    - E. The Chambre de la sécurité financière.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B, E
      Explanation: In Quebec, the AMF oversees fund companies and the right to practise, while the Chambre de la sécurité financière sets continuing education requirements and enforces a code of ethics.
    </details>

24. Which statement about the Investment Industry Regulatory Organization of Canada (IIROC) is correct?
    - A. Membership is optional for Canadian investment dealers.
    - B. It regulates mutual fund dealers exclusively.
    - C. All Canadian investment dealers must be members.
    - D. It regulates only the TSX Venture Exchange.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Every Canadian investment dealer must be an IIROC member. IIROC oversees investment dealers and trading activity, setting proficiency, business conduct, and market integrity rules.
    </details>

25. Unlike common shares, which trade in both the primary and secondary markets, mutual fund units are:
    - A. Traded mainly in the secondary market between investors.
    - B. Listed and traded on the Toronto Stock Exchange.
    - C. Traded exclusively on the Montreal Exchange.
    - D. Bought from and redeemed with the fund itself.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: Investors buy mutual fund units directly from the fund and redeem them with the fund, so they effectively trade only in the primary market.
    </details>
