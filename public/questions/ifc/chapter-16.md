# Chapter 16: Mutual Fund Fees and Services

Original practice questions grounded in the CSI Investment Funds in Canada (IFC) textbook, Chapter 16: sales charges and management fees, explicit and implicit costs (MER/TER), accumulation plans and dollar cost averaging, the five types of systematic withdrawal plans, and the taxation of mutual fund distributions and redemptions.

---

1. Mutual funds incur two broad types of fees. Sales charges (loads) are charged to individual investors when they buy or sell units, while management fees are:
    - A. Charged only when a fund is redeemed within the first year.
    - B. Illegal under Canadian securities law.
    - C. Paid directly out-of-pocket by the investor, separate from the fund.
    - D. Payable by the fund to its service providers and charged as expenses against the fund's earnings.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
    </details>

2. A sales charge paid at the time mutual fund units are purchased is called a front-end load, while a sales charge paid when units are redeemed is called a:
    - A. Trustee fee.
    - B. Back-end load, or deferred sales charge.
    - C. Trailer fee.
    - D. Set-up fee.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
    </details>

3. An ongoing annual fee, based on a percentage of assets, paid to a representative to service an existing client rather than to generate new sales, is called a:
    - A. Trailer fee.
    - B. Front-end load.
    - C. Redemption fee.
    - D. Account closing fee.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
    </details>

4. A client invests $1,000 in a mutual fund with a 4% front-end load. How much of that $1,000 is actually invested in fund units?
    - A. $1,000.
    - B. $1,040.
    - C. $960 (since $40, or 4% of $1,000, goes to the distributor).
    - D. $600.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
    </details>

5. A fund's NAVPS is $12, and it charges a 4% front-end load. What is the offering (purchase) price per unit?

    - B. $11.52.

6. Under a typical deferred sales charge schedule, the redemption fee percentage charged to an investor generally:
    - A. Increases the longer units are held.

10. The management expense ratio (MER), disclosed in the Fund Facts document and simplified prospectus, represents:
    - A. Only the fund's sales charges.
    - B. The annual total of all management fees and operating expenses, divided by average net assets.

    - D. A one-time charge paid at account opening.

    </details>

11. Which TWO of the following are among the factors described as affecting the size of a fund's MER? (Choose TWO)
    - A. The type of assets the fund invests in, since foreign equity mandates are typically more complex to manage than domestic T-bill mandates.
    - B. The client's personal marginal tax rate.
    - C. The size of the fund, since smaller funds must support their costs over a lower asset base.
    - D. The client's province of residence.
    - E. The number of units the client personally owns.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A, C
    </details>

12. A fund's MER is 2.42% and its trading expense ratio (TER) is 0.07%. What are the fund's total expenses, as presented in the Fund Facts document?

13. A fund's turnover rate measures:
    - A. The fund's total assets under management.

14. Brokerage commissions incurred when a fund buys and sells securities are considered:
    - A. Implicit costs, not included in the MER, though disclosed separately as the trading expense ratio.

15. According to the textbook, fund costs tend to have a greater relative impact on returns for which type of fund?
    - A. Aggressive small-cap growth equity funds.

17. The key benefit of dollar cost averaging, as illustrated by investing a fixed dollar amount monthly rather than in two lump sums, is that:
    - A. It guarantees a higher total return than lump-sum investing.
    - B. It tends to result in a lower average cost per unit over time and removes the need to time the market.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
    </details>

18. In a falling market, dollar cost averaging will generally result in:
    - A. A higher average cost per unit than if the investor had waited to invest at the market bottom, but still likely lower than investing a lump sum too early.
    - B. A guaranteed loss for the investor.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>

    </details>

19. Under a fixed-dollar (constant) withdrawal plan, the client:
    - A. Receives a fixed percentage of the fund's current value each period.
    - B. Receives the entire remaining balance divided by the years left in a set period.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
    </details>

20. Under a ratio withdrawal plan, because the withdrawal percentage is always applied to the current (fluctuating) portfolio value, technically:
    - A. The client's investment will always be exhausted within five years.
    - B. The withdrawal amount never changes from year to year.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
    </details>

21. Under a fixed-period withdrawal plan, each year's withdrawal is calculated as:
    - A. A fixed dollar amount set at the start of the plan.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>

    </details>

22. Which TWO of the following are true regarding a life withdrawal plan and an annuity? (Choose TWO)
    - A. A life withdrawal plan is similar to a fixed-period plan, but uses the client's expected remaining lifetime (based on mortality tables) as the period.
    - B. An annuity is a systematic withdrawal plan offered directly by the mutual fund itself, with no involvement from an insurance company.
    - C. A variable annuity always pays an identical fixed amount regardless of the underlying fund's performance.

    </details>

23. Unlike most annuities, which pay a fixed amount, a variable annuity's payments to the annuitant will:
