# Chapter 9: Understanding Financial Statements

Original practice questions grounded in the CSI Investment Funds in Canada (IFC) textbook, Chapter 9: the Statement of Financial Position, Statement of Comprehensive Income, Statement of Changes in Equity, the auditor's report, and ratio analysis (liquidity, risk, operating performance, and value ratios), including several calculations based on the textbook's own XYZ Inc. example.

---

1. The Statement of Financial Position for XYZ Inc. shows total assets of $520,000, total liabilities of $280,000, and shareholders' equity of $240,000. This illustrates the basic accounting equation:
    - A. Assets = Equity + Liabilities.
    - B. Assets = Revenue - Expenses.
    - C. Equity = Assets + Liabilities.
    - D. Liabilities = Assets + Equity.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
    </details>

2. XYZ Inc. reports cash, inventories, and trade receivables as current assets rather than fixed assets primarily because:
    - A. They are expected to last longer than one year.
    - B. They cannot be converted to cash under any circumstances.
    - C. They represent long-term debt obligations.
    - D. They are expected to be converted to cash within one year.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
    </details>

3. XYZ Inc. reports its plant and equipment "net" of an amount that reflects wear and tear or loss of usefulness over time. This adjustment is known as:
    - A. Retained earnings.
    - B. Depreciation or amortization.
    - C. Working capital.
    - D. Gross profit.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
    </details>

4. Using XYZ Inc.'s current assets of $120,000 and current liabilities of $80,000, what is the company's current ratio?
    - A. 0.67:1.
    - B. 1:1.
    - C. 1.5:1.
    - D. 2:1.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
    </details>

5. A representative notices that a company's current ratio has risen from 2:1 to 20:1 over several years. According to the textbook, this extremely high ratio might actually suggest:
    - A. An unnecessary accumulation of funds, possibly indicating sales problems or financial mismanagement.
    - B. Guaranteed financial strength with no downside.
    - C. That the company is at high risk of bankruptcy.
    - D. That the company has no current liabilities whatsoever.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
    </details>

6. The quick ratio (acid test) differs from the current ratio in that the quick ratio:
    - A. Adds inventory to current liabilities.
    - B. Includes fixed assets in the numerator.
    - C. Uses total assets instead of current assets.
    - D. Subtracts inventories from current assets, since inventory is not always readily convertible to cash.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>

    </details>

7. XYZ Inc. has total debt (notes payable plus long-term debt) of $240,000 and shareholders' equity of $240,000, giving a debt/equity ratio of 1:1. Given that creditors would normally hesitate to lend to companies exceeding a 0.50:1 ratio, this suggests XYZ:
    - A. Has no debt at all.
    - B. May be financed excessively by debt relative to what creditors would normally prefer.
    - C. Has a very conservative capital structure.
    - D. Cannot legally carry any further debt.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
    </details>

8. The interest coverage ratio is generally considered:
    - A. Irrelevant to assessing a company's debt securities.
    - B. A measure of a company's inventory efficiency.
    - C. The most important quantitative test of risk when considering a debt security, since it shows the margin of safety for interest payments.
    - D. Only applicable to equity securities.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
    </details>

9. XYZ Inc. reported profit of $55,000, taxes of $50,000, and interest expense of $20,000. What is XYZ's interest coverage ratio?

12. XYZ Inc. has a cost of sales of $600,000 and inventories of $60,000. Which TWO statements about XYZ's inventory turnover ratio are correct? (Choose TWO)
    - A. The inventory turnover ratio is 6 times.
    - B. XYZ turns over its inventory roughly once every 100 days.

17. As a general rule described in the textbook, P/E ratios tend to:
    - A. Increase in a rising stock market or when earnings are rising.
