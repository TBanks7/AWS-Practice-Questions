# Chapter 9: Understanding Financial Statements

Original practice questions grounded in the CSI Investment Funds in Canada (IFC) textbook, Chapter 9: the Statement of Financial Position, Statement of Comprehensive Income, Statement of Changes in Equity, the auditor's report, and ratio analysis (liquidity, risk, operating performance, and value ratios), including several calculations based on the textbook's XYZ Inc. example.

---

1. XYZ Inc. reports total assets of $520,000, total liabilities of $280,000, and shareholders' equity of $240,000. This illustrates the basic accounting equation:
    - A. Assets = Liabilities + Equity.
    - B. Assets = Revenue − Expenses.
    - C. Equity = Assets + Liabilities.
    - D. Liabilities = Assets + Equity.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: The Statement of Financial Position always balances: what the company owns (assets) equals what it owes (liabilities) plus the owners' stake (equity). $280,000 + $240,000 = $520,000.
    </details>

2. XYZ Inc. reports cash, inventories, and trade receivables as current assets because they are:
    - A. Expected to be used for more than one year.
    - B. Recorded at original cost less depreciation.
    - C. Financed through short-term borrowing.
    - D. Expected to become cash within one year.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: Current assets are cash and assets expected to be turned into cash within a year. Long-lived assets such as plant and equipment are fixed assets.
    </details>

3. XYZ Inc. reports its plant and equipment "net" of an amount reflecting wear and tear or loss of usefulness over time. This adjustment is known as:
    - A. Retained earnings.
    - B. Depreciation.
    - C. Working capital.
    - D. Goodwill.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: Depreciation (or amortization) spreads the cost of a long-lived asset over its useful life. It's a non-cash expense.
    </details>

4. XYZ Inc. has current assets of $120,000 and current liabilities of $80,000. What is its current ratio?
    - A. 0.67:1.
    - B. 1:1.
    - C. 1.5:1.
    - D. 0.75:1.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Current ratio = current assets / current liabilities = $120,000 / $80,000 = 1.5:1. The 0.75:1 figure is XYZ's quick ratio, which excludes inventory.
    </details>

5. A company's current ratio has risen from 2:1 to 20:1 over several years. According to the textbook, this very high ratio might actually suggest:
    - A. An unnecessary build-up of idle funds.
    - B. Financial strength with no downside.
    - C. A high risk of bankruptcy.
    - D. That the company has no current liabilities.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: A higher current ratio isn't always better. An extremely high one can mean idle cash or excess inventory, which may point to sales problems or poor financial management.
    </details>

6. The quick ratio (acid test) differs from the current ratio in that it:
    - A. Adds inventory to current liabilities.
    - B. Includes fixed assets in the numerator.
    - C. Uses total assets instead of current assets.
    - D. Excludes inventory from current assets.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: The quick ratio leaves out inventory because inventory can't always be turned into cash quickly. That makes it a stricter test of liquidity.
    </details>

7. XYZ Inc. has total debt (notes payable plus long-term debt) of $240,000 and equity of $240,000, a debt/equity ratio of 1:1. Since creditors generally hesitate to lend above 0.50:1, this suggests XYZ:
    - A. Has no debt at all.
    - B. May rely on debt more than creditors prefer.
    - C. Has a very conservative capital structure.
    - D. Cannot legally take on further debt.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: A 1:1 ratio is double the 0.50:1 comfort level, so XYZ may be carrying more debt than lenders would like. That raises its financial risk.
    </details>

8. The interest coverage ratio is generally considered:
    - A. Mainly relevant to equity investors.
    - B. A measure of inventory efficiency.
    - C. The key quantitative test of risk for a debt security.
    - D. Calculated using only current liabilities.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Interest coverage shows how many times earnings can cover interest payments. It's the main quantitative measure of the margin of safety for bondholders.
    </details>

9. XYZ Inc. reported profit of $55,000, taxes of $50,000, and interest expense of $20,000. What is its interest coverage ratio?
    - A. 6.25 times.
    - B. 2.75 times.
    - C. 3.75 times.
    - D. 5.25 times.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: Interest coverage = (profit + taxes + interest) / interest = ($55,000 + $50,000 + $20,000) / $20,000 = 6.25 times. Taxes and interest are added back to get earnings before interest and taxes.
    </details>

10. XYZ Inc. had revenue of $1,000,000 and cost of sales of $600,000. What is its gross profit margin?
    - A. 60%.
    - B. 5.5%.
    - C. 66.7%.
    - D. 40%.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: Gross profit margin = (revenue − cost of sales) / revenue = $400,000 / $1,000,000 = 40%. The 5.5% figure is XYZ's net profit margin.
    </details>

11. XYZ Inc. earned a profit of $55,000 on shareholders' equity of $240,000. What is its return on common equity, approximately?
    - A. 10.6%.
    - B. 22.9%.
    - C. 5.5%.
    - D. 19.6%.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: ROE = profit / shareholders' equity = $55,000 / $240,000 ≈ 22.9%. Dividing by total assets ($520,000) would give 10.6%, which is return on assets, not ROE.
    </details>

12. XYZ Inc. has a cost of sales of $600,000 and inventories of $60,000. Which TWO statements about its inventory turnover are correct? (Choose TWO)
    - A. The inventory turnover ratio is 16.7 times.
    - B. XYZ turns over its inventory about every 100 days.
    - C. The inventory turnover ratio is 10 times.
    - D. A high turnover ratio is always a bad sign.
    - E. XYZ turns over its inventory about every 36.5 days.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C, E
      Explanation: Inventory turnover = cost of sales / inventory = $600,000 / $60,000 = 10 times, or about every 365 / 10 = 36.5 days. Using revenue instead of cost of sales would give 16.7 times.
    </details>

13. XYZ Inc. earned a profit of $55,000 and has 100,000 common shares outstanding. What are its earnings per share?
    - A. $0.55.
    - B. $5.50.
    - C. $0.30.
    - D. $2.40.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: EPS = profit available to common shareholders / shares outstanding = $55,000 / 100,000 = $0.55. The $0.30 figure is the dividend per share.
    </details>

14. XYZ Inc.'s shares trade at $5, and its EPS is $0.55. What is its approximate price-earnings ratio?
    - A. 0.11 times.
    - B. 5.5 times.
    - C. 16.7 times.
    - D. 9.1 times.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: P/E = market price / EPS = $5 / $0.55 ≈ 9.1 times. Investors are paying about $9 for every $1 of earnings.
    </details>

15. Company A has EPS of $2 and trades at $20; Company B has EPS of $1 and trades at $10. Which statement correctly compares their valuations?
    - A. Company A is twice as expensive as Company B.
    - B. Both trade at the same P/E of 10 times.
    - C. Company B is overvalued relative to Company A.
    - D. P/E ratios can't be compared between companies.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: $20 / $2 = 10 and $10 / $1 = 10. The share prices differ, but investors are paying the same multiple of earnings for both companies.
    </details>

16. A stock pays an annual dividend of $0.30 per share and trades at $5.00. What is its dividend yield?
    - A. 0.3%.
    - B. 30%.
    - C. 6%.
    - D. 16.7%.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Dividend yield = annual dividend / share price = $0.30 / $5.00 = 6%.
    </details>

17. As a general rule described in the textbook, P/E ratios tend to:
    - A. Rise in a rising market or when earnings are rising.
    - B. Stay fixed regardless of market conditions.
    - C. Fall whenever the stock market rises.
    - D. Move independently of the overall market.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: When markets are rising and earnings are growing, investors are willing to pay more per dollar of earnings, so P/E ratios expand.
    </details>

18. A representative calculates a single ratio from a company's latest financial statements and concludes it's a strong investment. According to the textbook, a single-year ratio is:
    - A. Sufficient on its own to judge the company's health.
    - B. Of no analytical use under any circumstances.
    - C. Useful only when analyzing technology companies.
    - D. Of limited value unless compared over time or to peers.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: A ratio becomes meaningful when you compare it with the company's own past results (trend analysis) or with similar companies (external comparison).
    </details>

19. In trend analysis, an analyst sets a company's Year 1 earnings per share as the base and assigns it an index value of:
    - A. 0.
    - B. 100.
    - C. 1.
    - D. 10.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: The base year is set to 100, and later years are expressed relative to it. For example, 125 means a 25% increase over the base year.
    </details>

20. An analyst compares two companies' inventory turnover ratios, but one was calculated using cost of sales and the other using revenue. What is the problem?
    - A. None; the two figures are interchangeable.
    - B. Turnover ratios can never be compared across companies.
    - C. The comparison is invalid; the bases differ.
    - D. Revenue-based ratios are always more accurate.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Ratios can only be compared across companies when they're calculated on the same basis. Mixing cost of sales and revenue makes the comparison meaningless.
    </details>

21. Under Canadian corporate law, a limited company's financial statements generally:
    - A. Must be audited, unless all shareholders of a private company waive it.
    - B. Must be audited only if the company is government-owned.
    - C. Require external review only if revenue exceeds $1 million.
    - D. Must be audited by the company's own management.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: Companies must appoint an independent auditor to give an opinion on their financial statements. The exception is a private corporation whose shareholders all agree an audit isn't needed.
    </details>

22. XYZ Inc. began the year with retained earnings of $175,000, earned a profit of $55,000, and paid $30,000 in common dividends. What are its retained earnings at year-end?
    - A. $175,000.
    - B. $260,000.
    - C. $230,000.
    - D. $200,000.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: Closing retained earnings = opening + profit − dividends = $175,000 + $55,000 − $30,000 = $200,000. Forgetting the dividends gives $230,000.
    </details>

23. Which TWO of the following are among the four essential financial statements a corporation must produce? (Choose TWO)
    - A. The Fund Facts document.
    - B. The Statement of Financial Position.
    - C. The simplified prospectus.
    - D. The Statement of Comprehensive Income.
    - E. The auditor's report.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B, D
      Explanation: The four statements are the Statement of Financial Position, the Statement of Comprehensive Income, the Statement of Changes in Equity, and the Statement of Cash Flows. The auditor's report is an opinion on those statements, not a statement itself.
    </details>

24. Net profit margin is calculated as:
    - A. Gross profit divided by cost of sales.
    - B. Total assets divided by total liabilities.
    - C. Profit divided by revenue.
    - D. Profit divided by shareholders' equity.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Net profit margin = profit / revenue, which shows how much of each sales dollar ends up as profit. Profit divided by equity (option D) is ROE.
    </details>

25. A 2:1 current ratio might be excellent in one industry but only adequate in another. This is because:
    - A. Ratios must be compared within the same industry.
    - B. Current ratios are meaningless.
    - C. All industries require the same ratio.
    - D. Current ratios apply only to manufacturers.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: What a ratio means depends on the type of business, so the right benchmark is similar companies in the same industry.
    </details>

26. Financial statements of publicly traded Canadian companies are prepared under International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS), which are:
    - A. Rule-based, with minimal disclosure.
    - B. Optional for smaller public companies.
    - C. Used only in Canada.
    - D. Principle-based, with detailed disclosure.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: IFRS sets principles rather than detailed rules and relies on thorough disclosure. It's used in many countries, not just Canada.
    </details>

27. Cash flow from operations is generally calculated as profit, plus non-cash deductions (such as depreciation), minus:
    - A. Total shareholders' equity.
    - B. Additions to profit not received in cash.
    - C. Dividends declared during the year.
    - D. Total revenue.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: Cash flow from operations adjusts profit to a cash basis: add back non-cash charges and subtract income that hasn't been received in cash. Dividends are a financing item, not an operating adjustment.
    </details>

28. The gross profit margin is described as especially useful for industries such as food products and cosmetics because they have:
    - A. Little competition and stable pricing.
    - B. Very low current ratios.
    - C. High turnover and stiff competition.
    - D. No cost of sales.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: In high-turnover, highly competitive industries, small differences in how well cost of sales is managed make a big difference to profitability.
    </details>
