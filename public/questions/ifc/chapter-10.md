# Chapter 10: The Modern Mutual Fund

Original practice questions grounded in the CSI Investment Funds in Canada (IFC) textbook, Chapter 10: what a mutual fund is, its advantages and disadvantages, trust vs. corporate structure, fund governance and organization, mutual fund regulation (NI 81-101/81-102), and the Fund Facts, simplified prospectus, and Annual Information Form disclosure documents.

---

1. A mutual fund's investors share in its income, gains, losses, and expenses:
    - A. Equally among all investors.
    - B. In proportion to the units they own.
    - C. Only among the fund's original investors.
    - D. In proportion to how long they've held units.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: Each unitholder shares in the fund's results in proportion to the number of units or shares they own.
    </details>

2. Mutual fund units are redeemable on demand at:
    - A. The fund's offering price.
    - B. The units' par value.
    - C. The market bid price.
    - D. The net asset value per unit (NAVPU).

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: Units are bought and redeemed at NAVPS/NAVPU — the fund's net assets divided by the number of units outstanding — less any applicable charges.
    </details>

3. A retail investor with limited time, knowledge, and expertise to monitor a portfolio benefits most directly from a mutual fund's:
    - A. Professional management.
    - B. Guaranteed returns.
    - C. Use of leverage.
    - D. Deposit insurance.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: Low-cost professional management is a main advantage of mutual funds. Funds don't guarantee returns and aren't covered by deposit insurance.
    </details>

4. A large mutual fund might hold 60 to 100 or more securities across 15 to 20 industries. For an individual investor, building such a portfolio independently is generally:
    - A. Simple and inexpensive.
    - B. Required by provincial regulators.
    - C. Impractical for most individual investors.
    - D. Possible only through a segregated fund.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Most individuals don't have the capital to build that level of diversification themselves. Pooling money in a fund makes it achievable.
    </details>

5. A pre-authorized contribution plan (PAC) typically allows an investor to build a mutual fund investment with:
    - A. A $5,000 minimum lump sum.
    - B. A $1,000 minimum lump sum.
    - C. Annual contributions only.
    - D. As little as $100 per month.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: PACs let investors start small, often with as little as $100 and then $100 a month, which makes regular investing easy.
    </details>

6. National Instrument 81-102 requires that mutual fund redemption proceeds be paid:
    - A. Within 30 days of the redemption request.
    - B. Within two business days of the pricing date.
    - C. Within five business days of the request.
    - D. Within one year of the redemption request.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: Redemption proceeds must be paid within two business days after the date the redemption price (NAV) is determined. This is a key part of mutual fund liquidity.
    </details>

7. A fund manager sells a security at a profit to fit the fund's strategy, though an individual unitholder would have preferred to defer the capital gains tax. This illustrates which disadvantage of mutual funds?
    - A. Lack of diversification.
    - B. Illiquidity.
    - C. Tax complications.
    - D. Loss of professional management.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: The manager trades for the fund as a whole, so the timing of taxable distributions may not suit any particular unitholder's tax situation.
    </details>

8. With the exception of money market funds, mutual funds are generally NOT recommended as:
    - A. An emergency cash reserve.
    - B. A retirement savings vehicle.
    - C. A way to diversify.
    - D. A source of professional management.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: Most fund values fluctuate, so a client who has to redeem in an emergency could be forced to sell during a decline. Money market funds are the exception.
    </details>

9. The open-end trust structure allows the fund itself to avoid paying tax because:
    - A. Trusts are exempt from the Income Tax Act.
    - B. Only corporations pay tax in Canada.
    - C. Mutual fund trusts never earn taxable income.
    - D. Income is distributed and taxed in unitholders' hands.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: A mutual fund trust distributes its income to unitholders each year, and the income is taxed in their hands rather than in the trust's.
    </details>

10. The declaration of trust (trust deed) that establishes a mutual fund trust sets out all of the following EXCEPT:
    - A. The fund's investment objectives and policy.
    - B. Each unitholder's KYC information.
    - C. The identity of the trustee, manager, and custodian.
    - D. The classes or series of units offered.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: The trust deed defines how the fund works — its objectives, restrictions, key parties, and unit classes. KYC information is collected by the dealer, not recorded in the trust deed.
    </details>

11. Under NI 81-102, which TWO of the following changes generally require the approval of unitholders at a meeting? (Choose TWO)
    - A. A change in the fund's fundamental investment objectives.
    - B. A routine daily NAV calculation.
    - C. An increase in a fee or expense charged to the fund.
    - D. A change in the fund's portfolio holdings.
    - E. An individual unitholder's redemption request.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A, C
      Explanation: Unitholder approval is needed for major changes, such as a new fundamental objective, a fee increase, a change of manager, or less frequent NAV calculation. Buying and selling holdings is ordinary management.
    </details>

12. Unlike mutual fund trusts, which issue units, mutual fund corporations issue:
    - A. Units.
    - B. Debentures.
    - C. Contracts.
    - D. Shares.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: Mutual fund corporations issue shares to shareholders. They get a result similar to a trust's flow-through by paying out income as dividends.
    </details>

13. Which statement about a mutual fund manager is correct?
    - A. It may also act as the fund's custodian.
    - B. It may not act as the fund's custodian.
    - C. It isn't responsible for portfolio managers it hires.
    - D. It may not hire any third parties.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: The manager can't act as custodian, which keeps the fund's assets separate from the manager. It can hire portfolio managers and other service providers but stays responsible for them.
    </details>

14. A fund manager's management fee is typically:
    - A. Accrued daily and paid monthly, as a percentage of net assets.
    - B. A fixed dollar amount set when the fund is launched.
    - C. Paid directly by each unitholder at purchase.
    - D. Charged only when units are redeemed.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: The management fee is a percentage of the fund's net asset value, calculated and accrued daily and paid monthly in arrears. It's charged to the fund, not paid directly by unitholders.
    </details>

15. Under National Instrument 81-107, every mutual fund must have an Independent Review Committee whose role is to:
    - A. Manage the fund's investment portfolio.
    - B. Set the fund's management expense ratio.
    - C. Review conflicts of interest referred by the manager.
    - D. Hold the fund's cash and securities.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: The IRC reviews or approves conflicts of interest the manager identifies, to make sure they're resolved fairly for the fund.
    </details>

16. A mutual fund's custodian is primarily responsible for:
    - A. Making the fund's investment decisions.
    - B. Reviewing conflicts of interest.
    - C. Selling the fund's units to the public.
    - D. Holding the fund's cash and securities.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: The custodian receives and holds the fund's money and assets and settles its transactions. Investment decisions belong to the portfolio manager, and conflicts go to the IRC.
    </details>

17. The MFDA regulates how mutual funds are sold, but it does NOT regulate:
    - A. Member dealers' representatives.
    - B. The mutual funds themselves.
    - C. Sales practices of member dealers.
    - D. Member dealers' capital requirements.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: The MFDA covers the distribution side — dealers and their representatives. The funds themselves are regulated by the provincial securities commissions.
    </details>

18. National Instrument 81-101 deals primarily with mutual fund prospectus disclosure, while National Instrument 81-102 deals primarily with:
    - A. Mutual fund operations and sales communications.
    - B. The registration of dealing representatives.
    - C. Independent review committees for funds.
    - D. Continuous disclosure for public companies.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: NI 81-102 governs how mutual funds operate and are distributed and advertised. Registration falls under NI 31-103 and IRCs under NI 81-107.
    </details>

19. The Fund Facts document must be written in plain language and may not exceed:
    - A. One page.
    - B. Ten pages.
    - C. Two double-sided pages.
    - D. Twenty pages.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Fund Facts is limited to two double-sided pages (four pages) so investors can quickly see the key information.
    </details>

20. Which TWO of the following investor rights must be disclosed in the Fund Facts document? (Choose TWO)
    - A. A right to a guaranteed minimum annual return.
    - B. A right to withdraw within 48 hours of receiving confirmation.
    - C. A right to a full refund at any time, with no time limit.
    - D. A right to rescission or damages for a misrepresentation.
    - E. A right to vote on the fund's portfolio holdings.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B, D
      Explanation: Fund Facts tells investors they can withdraw from a purchase within 48 hours of receiving the trade confirmation and, depending on the province, may have rights of rescission or damages if the document contains a misrepresentation.
    </details>

21. The Fund Facts document places a fund in one of how many risk rating categories, from Low to High?
    - A. Five.
    - B. Three.
    - C. Seven.
    - D. Ten.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: The five categories are Low, Low-to-Medium, Medium, Medium-to-High, and High.
    </details>

22. Under NI 81-101, which document must be delivered to an investor before or at the point of purchase?
    - A. The Annual Information Form.
    - B. The annual financial statements.
    - C. The Fund Facts document.
    - D. The simplified prospectus.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Fund Facts must be delivered before or at the point of sale. The simplified prospectus and AIF are provided on request.
    </details>

23. The simplified prospectus has two parts. Part B:
    - A. Is identical to the Fund Facts document.
    - B. Contains the auditor's report only.
    - C. Describes the fund family and mutual funds in general.
    - D. Contains information specific to each fund.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: Part A covers general information about the fund family and mutual funds. Part B covers each specific fund.
    </details>

24. Unlike the Fund Facts document, the simplified prospectus must be delivered:
    - A. Before every purchase.
    - B. On request.
    - C. Only at year-end.
    - D. Only to institutional investors.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: Since Fund Facts became the required point-of-sale document, the simplified prospectus is provided only when the investor asks for it.
    </details>

25. The Management Expense Ratio (MER) and Trading Expense Ratio (TER), disclosed in the Fund Facts document, both represent:
    - A. Costs that aren't paid directly but reduce returns.
    - B. Fees paid out of pocket at the time of purchase.
    - C. Charges applied only when units are redeemed.
    - D. Fees that don't affect the fund's net returns.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: MER and TER are paid by the fund, not charged directly to the investor, but they reduce the investor's returns.
    </details>

26. Which of the following would generally be unable to use the simplified prospectus system under NI 81-101?
    - A. A balanced fund.
    - B. A Canadian equity fund.
    - C. A fund that invests in real property.
    - D. A money market fund.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Certain funds, such as those investing in real property, can't use the simplified prospectus system and must file a full prospectus.
    </details>

27. Compared with the simplified prospectus, the Annual Information Form (AIF) additionally discloses:
    - A. Nothing; the two documents are identical.
    - B. Each unitholder's KYC profile.
    - C. The fund's daily NAV history.
    - D. Significant holdings and directors' remuneration.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: The AIF adds details not in the simplified prospectus, such as significant holdings in other issuers and the indebtedness and pay of directors, officers, and trustees.
    </details>

28. In the case study, Daniella wants equity exposure but lacks the capital, time, and knowledge to buy the 25 to 30 stocks needed for proper diversification. A mutual fund suits her mainly because it offers:
    - A. A guaranteed return regardless of market conditions.
    - B. Diversification and professional management at a low minimum.
    - C. Protection from all market risk at a low minimum.
    - D. Higher returns than any individual stock portfolio.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: A fund gives Daniella instant diversification and professional management without needing a large sum. It doesn't eliminate market risk or guarantee returns.
    </details>
