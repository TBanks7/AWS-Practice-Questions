# Chapter 10: The Modern Mutual Fund

Original practice questions grounded in the CSI Investment Funds in Canada (IFC) textbook, Chapter 10: what a mutual fund is, its advantages and disadvantages, trust vs. corporate structure, fund governance and organization, mutual fund regulation (NI 81-101/81-102), and the Fund Facts, simplified prospectus, and Annual Information Form disclosure documents.

---

1. A mutual fund pools money from investors, who become unitholders or shareholders and share in the fund's income, gains, losses and expenses. This sharing occurs:
    - A. Equally among all investors regardless of investment size.
    - B. In proportion to the number of units or shares each investor owns.
    - C. Only among investors who joined the fund in its first year.
    - D. According to a lottery system determined annually.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
    </details>

2. Mutual fund shares or units are redeemable on demand at the fund's current price, known as:
    - A. The prospectus price.
    - B. The par value.
    - C. The bid-ask spread.
    - D. The net asset value per share (NAVPS) or net asset value per unit (NAVPU).

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
    </details>

3. A retail investor with limited time, knowledge, and expertise to actively monitor a securities portfolio benefits most directly from a mutual fund's:
    - A. Low-cost professional management.
    - B. Guaranteed principal protection.
    - C. Unlimited leverage capability.
    - D. Exemption from all regulatory oversight.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
    </details>

4. A typical large mutual fund might hold 60 to 100 or more securities across 15 to 20 industries. For an individual investor, acquiring such a portfolio independently is generally:
    - A. Simple and inexpensive.
    - B. Required by every provincial securities regulator.
    - C. Not feasible, making pooled fund ownership a practical way to access a diversified portfolio.
    - D. Only possible through a segregated fund.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
    </details>

5. A pre-authorized contribution plan (PAC) allows an investor to begin building a mutual fund investment with:
    - A. A minimum lump sum of $50,000.
    - B. No minimum contribution amount permitted by any dealer.
    - C. Only annual contributions.
    - D. As little as $100 to start, and as little as $100 per month thereafter.

    </details>

6. National Instrument 81-102 requires that mutual fund redemption payments be made to the unitholder within:
    - A. 30 calendar days after the redemption request.
    - B. Two business days after the date the net asset value used to calculate the redemption price is determined.
    - C. One year after the redemption request.
    - D. There is no required timeline.

7. A fund manager sells a security at a profit that suits the fund's overall strategy, even though an individual unitholder might have preferred deferring the resulting capital gains tax. This scenario illustrates which disadvantage of mutual fund ownership?
    - A. Lack of diversification.
    - B. Illiquidity.
    - C. Tax complications, since the manager's trading decisions may not align with an individual unitholder's tax timing preferences.
    - D. Guaranteed capital loss.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
    </details>

8. With the exception of money market funds, mutual fund holdings are generally NOT recommended as:
    - A. An emergency cash reserve, particularly during declining markets.
    - B. A long-term retirement savings vehicle.
    - C. A way to achieve diversification.
    - D. A vehicle for professional management.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>

    </details>

9. The open-end trust structure, the most common mutual fund structure in Canada, allows the fund itself to avoid taxation because:
    - A. Trusts are exempt from the Income Tax Act entirely.
    - B. Only corporations are subject to tax in Canada.
    - C. Mutual fund trusts never earn any taxable income.
    - D. Income is passed through to unitholders each year and taxed in their hands instead of the trust's.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>

    </details>

10. The declaration of trust (or trust deed) establishing a mutual fund trust sets out all of the following EXCEPT:
    - A. The fund's principal investment objectives and investment policy.

11. Under NI 81-102, which TWO of the following changes generally require the trustee or manager to convene a meeting of unitholders for approval? (Choose TWO)

13. Within a mutual fund's organizational structure, which statement about the fund manager is correct?

14. A fund manager's management fee is typically:
    - A. Calculated and accrued daily, then paid monthly in arrears, as a percentage of the fund's net asset value.
