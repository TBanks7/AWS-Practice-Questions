# Chapter 13: Alternative Managed Products

Original practice questions grounded in the CSI Investment Funds in Canada (IFC) textbook, Chapter 13: Principal-Protected Notes, hedge funds and alternative mutual funds, closed-end funds, exchange-traded funds, and segregated funds.

---

1. A Principal-Protected Note (PPN) is best classified as a:
    - A. Debt instrument with a maturity date at which the issuer guarantees repayment of principal, plus interest linked to an underlying asset's performance.
    - B. Type of open-end mutual fund trust.
    - C. Segregated fund insurance contract.
    - D. Exchange-traded equity security.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
    </details>

2. In Canada, PPNs are issued only by the six major banks, which act in three main roles. Which of the following correctly lists those three roles?
    - A. Custodian, trustee, and portfolio manager.
    - B. Regulator, auditor, and distributor.
    - C. Underwriter, dealer, and fund manager.
    - D. Guarantor, manufacturer, and distributor.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
    </details>

3. Regarding deposit insurance, PPNs are:
    - A. Fully covered by CDIC, just like a GIC.
    - B. Considered uninsured deposit notes, not protected by CDIC.
    - C. Covered by provincial deposit insurance only.
    - D. Automatically covered once held for more than five years.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
    </details>

4. A PPN's final payoff is based on the note's average NAV over its life rather than the underlying asset's value at maturity. This feature, known as a performance averaging formula, is best described as a(n):
    - A. Explicit cost disclosed prominently on the front page of the offering document.
    - B. Guarantee of a higher return than holding the underlying asset directly.
    - C. Implicit cost that may raise or lower the investor's actual return compared to the underlying asset's point-in-time performance.
    - D. Feature exclusive to segregated fund contracts.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
    </details>

5. An index-linked GIC promises to pay 60% of the return of the S&P/TSX 60 Index while guaranteeing principal. If the index's actual return exceeds what 60% would produce, the investor:
    - A. Incurs an opportunity cost, since they cannot capture the return above the cap.
    - B. Automatically receives the full index return regardless of the cap.
    - C. Loses part of their principal.
    - D. Receives double the capped return as compensation.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
    </details>

6. An investor who redeems a PPN before its maturity date will typically:
    - A. Still receive the full guaranteed principal amount.
    - B. Receive a bonus for redeeming early.
    - C. Automatically convert the PPN into a segregated fund.
    - D. Lose the principal guarantee and receive the note's current resale value, which the issuer is under no obligation to provide.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
    </details>

7. Compared to conventional mutual funds, hedge fund managers generally have:
    - A. Less flexibility in the investment strategies they may use.
    - B. Much greater flexibility, since they are not constrained by the same rules that apply to mutual funds.
    - C. No ability to use derivatives or short positions.
    - D. Identical regulatory requirements to conventional mutual funds.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
    </details>

8. Unlike hedge funds, which have a limited audience of eligible investors, alternative mutual funds (liquid alts):
    - A. Cannot be sold to any investors under any circumstances.
    - B. Are subject to identical exemption requirements as hedge funds.
    - C. Can be sold to retail investors, while allowing greater use of leverage, short sales, and derivatives than conventional mutual funds.
    - D. Are entirely prohibited from using derivatives.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>

    </details>

9. Under National Instrument 45-106, an individual generally qualifies as an accredited investor if their net income before taxes exceeded which amount in each of the two most recent years (with a reasonable expectation of exceeding it again this year)?
    - A. $200,000 (or $300,000 combined with a spouse).

    - C. $1,000.
    - D. $10,000.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>

    </details>

10. National Instrument 45-106 sets the minimum investment exemption, which is not available to retail investors, at:
    - A. $10,000.

11. Hedge fund strategies are generally grouped into three categories, listed in order of INCREASING expected return and risk. Which of the following correctly reflects that order?
    - A. Directional, event-driven, relative value.
    - B. Relative value, event-driven, directional.
    - C. Event-driven, relative value, directional.
    - D. All three strategy types carry identical risk and expected return.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
    </details>

12. Which TWO of the following statements about hedge fund incentive fees are correct? (Choose TWO)
    - A. A high-water mark allows a manager to charge an incentive fee on losses recovered up to any previous value, even below the fund's historical peak.
    - B. Incentive fees are always calculated on the fund's gross return, before management fees and expenses are deducted.
    - C. A high-water mark ensures a manager is paid an incentive fee only on net new profits above the fund's previous highest value.
    - D. A hurdle rate guarantees the manager an incentive fee regardless of the fund's performance.
    - E. A hurdle rate is the minimum return a hedge fund must earn before its manager becomes eligible for an incentive fee.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C, E
    </details>

13. A hedge fund manager profits by exploiting pricing inefficiencies between related bonds in different markets, with little exposure to overall market direction. This describes a:
    - A. Relative value strategy.
    - B. Directional strategy.
    - C. Event-driven strategy exclusively focused on mergers.
    - D. Passive index-tracking strategy.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
    </details>

14. Unlike a conventional open-end mutual fund, a closed-end fund:
    - A. Continuously issues and redeems units on demand at NAV.
    - B. Cannot be traded by investors at all.
    - C. Must always trade at its exact net asset value.
    - D. Has a fixed number of shares that trade on a stock exchange, except in rare cases such as an additional offering or share buy-back.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
    </details>

15. A closed-end fund's market price is currently below the combined net asset value of its underlying holdings. This gap is known as the fund trading at a:
    - A. Premium.
    - B. Par value.
    - C. Discount to NAV.
    - D. Hurdle rate.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
    </details>

16. Because a closed-end fund does not need to keep cash on hand to meet unpredictable redemptions, it can generally:
    - A. Never use any leverage.
    - B. Remain closer to fully invested than a comparable open-end mutual fund.
    - C. Only invest in money market securities.
    - D. Avoid all forms of market risk.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
    </details>

17. An Exchange-Traded Fund (ETF) is best described as a:
    - A. Basket of securities constructed like a mutual fund but traded on an exchange like an individual stock.
    - B. Type of insurance contract with a maturity guarantee.
    - C. Fund that can only be redeemed directly from the fund company.
    - D. Debt instrument guaranteeing full principal repayment.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
    </details>

18. The degree to which an ETF's returns fail to precisely mirror its target index's returns is known as:
    - A. A high-water mark.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>

    </details>

19. Because ETFs are not subject to the same individual stock or sector exposure limits that apply to conventional mutual funds, they may be exposed to:
    - A. Zero market risk of any kind.

    </details>

20. Unlike an index mutual fund, which must hold some cash to meet potential redemptions, an ETF generally:
