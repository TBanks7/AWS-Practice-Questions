# Chapter 13: Alternative Managed Products

Original practice questions grounded in the CSI Investment Funds in Canada (IFC) textbook, Chapter 13: Principal-Protected Notes, hedge funds and alternative mutual funds, closed-end funds, exchange-traded funds, and segregated funds.

---

1. A Principal-Protected Note (PPN) is best classified as:
    - A. A debt instrument with a principal guarantee at maturity.
    - B. An open-end mutual fund trust.
    - C. An insurance contract with a death benefit.
    - D. An exchange-traded equity security.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: A PPN is a debt instrument. The issuer guarantees repayment of principal at maturity and pays a return tied to the performance of an underlying asset.
    </details>

2. In Canada, PPNs are issued by the six major banks, which act in three roles. They are:
    - A. Custodian, trustee, and portfolio manager.
    - B. Regulator, auditor, and distributor.
    - C. Underwriter, dealer, and fund manager.
    - D. Guarantor, manufacturer, and distributor.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: The bank guarantees the principal, designs (manufactures) the note, and distributes it — which can create conflicts of interest.
    </details>

3. Regarding deposit insurance, PPNs are:
    - A. Fully CDIC-covered, just like a GIC.
    - B. Not covered by CDIC.
    - C. Covered by provincial insurance only.
    - D. Covered once held for five years.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: PPNs are uninsured deposit notes. The principal guarantee depends entirely on the issuing bank's creditworthiness.
    </details>

4. A PPN's payoff is based on the note's average value over its life rather than the underlying asset's value at maturity. This performance averaging formula is best described as:
    - A. An explicit cost shown on the front page.
    - B. A guarantee of higher returns than the asset.
    - C. An implicit cost that can alter the investor's return.
    - D. A feature found only in segregated funds.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Averaging features are implicit costs. They aren't obvious from the fee disclosure but can make the investor's return quite different from the underlying asset's.
    </details>

5. An index-linked GIC pays 60% of the S&P/TSX 60 Index's return while guaranteeing principal. If the index rises strongly, the investor:
    - A. Gives up the return above the participation rate.
    - B. Receives the full index return anyway.
    - C. Loses part of their principal.
    - D. Receives double the capped return.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: The participation rate limits the upside. The investor gets 60% of the gain, and missing out on the rest is an opportunity cost of the principal guarantee.
    </details>

6. An investor who redeems a PPN before its maturity date will typically:
    - A. Still receive the full guaranteed principal.
    - B. Receive a bonus for redeeming early.
    - C. Have the note converted into a mutual fund.
    - D. Lose the guarantee and get the resale value.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: The guarantee applies only at maturity. Before then, the investor gets whatever the note can be sold for, which may be less than they paid, and the issuer isn't required to buy it back.
    </details>

7. Compared with conventional mutual fund managers, hedge fund managers generally have:
    - A. Less flexibility in their strategies.
    - B. Much greater flexibility in their strategies.
    - C. No ability to use derivatives.
    - D. The same regulatory limits.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: Hedge funds aren't bound by mutual fund investment restrictions, so they can use leverage, short selling, and derivatives freely.
    </details>

8. Unlike hedge funds, which have a limited audience of eligible investors, alternative mutual funds (liquid alts):
    - A. Can be sold only to accredited investors.
    - B. Face the same exemptions as hedge funds.
    - C. Can be sold to retail investors.
    - D. Are barred from using any derivatives.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Liquid alts are sold by prospectus to retail investors. They can use more leverage, short selling, and derivatives than conventional mutual funds, but less than hedge funds.
    </details>

9. Under NI 45-106, an individual generally qualifies as an accredited investor if their net income before taxes in each of the two most recent years exceeded:
    - A. $200,000 ($300,000 with a spouse).
    - B. $100,000 ($150,000 with a spouse).
    - C. $150,000 ($250,000 with a spouse).
    - D. $1,000,000 ($1,500,000 with a spouse).

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: The income test is $200,000 individually or $300,000 combined with a spouse, with a reasonable expectation of exceeding it again this year. Other tests are based on financial assets or net assets.
    </details>

10. NI 45-106 sets the minimum investment exemption, which is not available to individuals, at:
    - A. $10,000.
    - B. $1,000,000.
    - C. $5,000,000.
    - D. $150,000.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: The minimum investment exemption is $150,000. The $1 million and $5 million figures are thresholds in the accredited investor tests.
    </details>

11. Which sequence lists hedge fund strategy categories in order of INCREASING expected return and risk?
    - A. Directional, event-driven, relative value.
    - B. Relative value, event-driven, directional.
    - C. Event-driven, relative value, directional.
    - D. Relative value, directional, event-driven.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: Relative value strategies exploit pricing gaps with little market exposure, event-driven strategies bet on corporate events, and directional strategies take outright market bets, which carry the most risk.
    </details>

12. Which TWO of the following statements about hedge fund incentive fees are correct? (Choose TWO)
    - A. A high-water mark lets the manager earn fees on recovering past losses.
    - B. Incentive fees are always based on gross returns.
    - C. A high-water mark limits incentive fees to new profits above the prior peak.
    - D. A hurdle rate guarantees the manager an incentive fee.
    - E. A hurdle rate is the minimum return required before an incentive fee is earned.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C, E
      Explanation: A high-water mark means no incentive fee is paid for simply recovering losses — only on gains above the previous peak. A hurdle rate sets a minimum return the fund must beat before any incentive fee applies.
    </details>

13. A hedge fund profits from pricing differences between related bonds in different markets, with little exposure to overall market direction. This is a:
    - A. Relative value strategy.
    - B. Directional strategy.
    - C. Event-driven strategy.
    - D. Passive index strategy.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: Relative value strategies profit from mispricing between related securities rather than from the market's direction, which makes them the least risky hedge fund category.
    </details>

14. Unlike a conventional open-end mutual fund, a closed-end fund:
    - A. Continuously issues and redeems units at NAV.
    - B. Can't be traded by investors at all.
    - C. Always trades at its exact NAV.
    - D. Has a fixed number of shares traded on an exchange.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: A closed-end fund issues a fixed number of shares that investors trade on an exchange. New shares are issued only in rare cases, such as an additional offering.
    </details>

15. A closed-end fund's market price is below the combined value of its underlying holdings. The fund is trading at a:
    - A. Premium to NAV.
    - B. Par value.
    - C. Discount to NAV.
    - D. Bid-ask spread.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Because closed-end fund shares trade on an exchange, supply, demand, and market sentiment can push the price below NAV (a discount) or above it (a premium).
    </details>

16. Because a closed-end fund doesn't need cash on hand to meet unpredictable redemptions, it can generally:
    - A. Never use leverage.
    - B. Stay more fully invested.
    - C. Invest only in money market securities.
    - D. Avoid market risk.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: Investors sell closed-end shares to other investors rather than redeeming them from the fund, so the fund can stay close to fully invested.
    </details>

17. An Exchange-Traded Fund (ETF) is best described as:
    - A. A basket of securities traded on an exchange like a stock.
    - B. An insurance contract with a maturity guarantee.
    - C. A fund redeemable only with the fund company.
    - D. A debt instrument with principal protection.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: An ETF holds a basket of securities, usually tracking an index, and trades on an exchange throughout the day like a stock.
    </details>

18. The degree to which an ETF's returns fail to match its target index is known as:
    - A. A high-water mark.
    - B. A hurdle rate.
    - C. Market sentiment.
    - D. Tracking error.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: Tracking error is the gap between the ETF's return and its index's return, caused by fees, cash holdings, and imperfect replication.
    </details>

19. Because ETFs aren't subject to the same holding limits as conventional mutual funds, they may be exposed to:
    - A. No market risk.
    - B. Guaranteed sector diversification.
    - C. Concentration risk.
    - D. Immunity from currency risk.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: An ETF tracking a narrow index may hold more than 10% in one stock or more than 40% in one sector. Mutual fund rules would normally prevent that level of concentration.
    </details>

20. Unlike an index mutual fund, which must keep some cash to meet redemptions, an ETF generally:
    - A. Must hold more cash than an index mutual fund.
    - B. Can stay more fully invested.
    - C. Is prohibited from holding cash.
    - D. Trades only once a day.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: ETF investors trade with each other on the exchange rather than redeeming from the fund, so there is less cash drag.
    </details>

21. Which TWO of the following are among the three parties to a segregated fund contract? (Choose TWO)
    - A. The contract holder.
    - B. The custodian.
    - C. The portfolio manager.
    - D. The beneficiary.
    - E. The trustee.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A, D
      Explanation: The three parties are the contract holder (who buys it), the annuitant (whose life the benefits are based on), and the beneficiary (who receives the death benefit).
    </details>

22. Provincial legislation requires a segregated fund's maturity guarantee to be at least what percentage of the amount invested, over a minimum 10-year holding period?
    - A. 50%.
    - B. 60%.
    - C. 75%.
    - D. 100%.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: The legal minimum is 75%. Many contracts offer 100%, but usually at a higher cost.
    </details>

23. A segregated fund contract guarantees $10,000. At the annuitant's death, the units are worth $8,000. What death benefit, in addition to the market value, will the beneficiary receive?
    - A. $10,000.
    - B. $2,000.
    - C. $8,000.
    - D. $0.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: The death benefit tops up the market value to the guaranteed amount: $10,000 − $8,000 = $2,000, for a total payout of $10,000.
    </details>

24. A key advantage of segregated funds not generally available with conventional mutual funds is:
    - A. Guaranteed outperformance.
    - B. No management fees.
    - C. No minimum holding period.
    - D. Potential creditor protection.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: The insurance company owns the fund's assets, so with a properly designated beneficiary the contract may be protected from the holder's creditors.
    </details>

25. Because segregated fund proceeds go directly to a named beneficiary rather than into the estate, segregated funds can help investors:
    - A. Bypass probate.
    - B. Avoid income tax permanently.
    - C. Avoid needing a will.
    - D. Receive a 150% death benefit.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: Paying the proceeds directly to the beneficiary avoids the costs, delays, and publicity of probate.
    </details>

26. Because segregated funds are insurance contracts rather than securities, selling them requires:
    - A. No licence of any kind.
    - B. A mutual fund licence only.
    - C. A life insurance licence.
    - D. A hedge fund designation.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Segregated funds are regulated as insurance, so only licensed life insurance representatives can sell them. A mutual fund licence alone isn't enough.
    </details>

27. A Guaranteed Minimum Withdrawal Benefit (GMWB) plan lets the planholder withdraw a fixed percentage of the initial deposit each year, typically around:
    - A. 100%.
    - B. 7%.
    - C. 50%.
    - D. 2%.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: GMWB plans typically guarantee annual withdrawals of about 7% of the initial deposit until the full amount is returned, whatever the markets do.
    </details>

28. GMWB plans are described as especially suitable for clients who:
    - A. Plan to withdraw everything immediately.
    - B. Don't want to share in market growth.
    - C. Are more than 30 years from retirement.
    - D. Are 5 to 10 years from retirement.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: Clients close to retirement can't afford big losses right before they need income. A GMWB guarantees withdrawals while still letting them share in market growth.
    </details>
