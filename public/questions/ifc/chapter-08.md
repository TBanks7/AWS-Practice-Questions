# Chapter 8: Constructing Investment Portfolios

Original practice questions grounded in the CSI Investment Funds in Canada (IFC) textbook, Chapter 8: risk and return, inflation and taxation, calculating returns (arithmetic vs. geometric mean, portfolio return), measuring risk (variance, standard deviation, beta, alpha, duration), diversification and correlation, portfolio management styles, strategic asset allocation, and fundamental vs. technical analysis.

---

1. The expected return on a single security is calculated as expected cash flow plus expected capital gain (or minus capital loss), divided by:
    - A. The expected ending value.
    - B. The beginning value of the investment.
    - C. The standard deviation of returns.
    - D. The rate of inflation.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: Return is always measured relative to what was invested: (income + price change) / beginning value.
    </details>

2. A client earned a nominal return of 6% last year while inflation was 2.5%. Approximately what real rate of return did the client earn?
    - A. 8.5%.
    - B. 2.5%.
    - C. 6.0%.
    - D. 3.5%.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: Real return ≈ nominal return − inflation = 6% − 2.5% = 3.5%.
    </details>

3. If a client's nominal return exactly equals the inflation rate for the year, the client's purchasing power has been:
    - A. Maintained, but with no real increase in wealth.
    - B. Increased by the amount of the nominal return.
    - C. Reduced by the amount of inflation.
    - D. Increased by half the nominal return.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: When nominal return equals inflation, the real return is 0%. The client can buy exactly what they could before, but no more.
    </details>

4. Of the following types of investment income, which is generally taxed most heavily for a Canadian investor?
    - A. Capital gains.
    - B. Eligible Canadian dividends.
    - C. Interest income.
    - D. All are taxed equally.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Interest is fully taxed at the marginal rate. Only half of capital gains is taxable, and eligible dividends get the dividend tax credit.
    </details>

5. An investor bought a stock for $20, received $1 in dividends during the year, and sold it a year later for $22. What was the rate of return?
    - A. 5%.
    - B. 15%.
    - C. 10%.
    - D. 11%.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: Return = (income + capital gain) / beginning value = ($1 + $2) / $20 = 15%.
    </details>

6. A fund's five-year returns were highly volatile, with both large gains and losses. Comparing the arithmetic mean and geometric mean of these returns:
    - A. The geometric mean will be higher.
    - B. Both means will be identical.
    - C. The arithmetic mean can't be calculated.
    - D. The arithmetic mean will be higher.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: The geometric mean is always less than or equal to the arithmetic mean. The two are equal only when every period's return is the same, and the gap widens as returns become more volatile.
    </details>

7. A client wants to know the actual compound growth rate their money achieved over a five-year holding period. The representative should use the fund's:
    - A. Geometric mean return.
    - B. Arithmetic mean return.
    - C. Beta.
    - D. Standard deviation.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: The geometric mean reflects compounding and the actual change in wealth over the period. The arithmetic mean overstates it when returns are volatile.
    </details>

8. A client invests $60 in Fund A, expected to return 15%, and $40 in Fund B, expected to return 12%. What is the portfolio's expected return?
    - A. 27.0%.
    - B. 12.0%.
    - C. 13.8%.
    - D. 13.5%.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Portfolio return is the weighted average: (0.60 × 15%) + (0.40 × 12%) = 9% + 4.8% = 13.8%. The simple average (13.5%) ignores the weights.
    </details>

9. Two funds both averaged a 12% annual return over five years. Fund X has a standard deviation of 10% and Fund Y a standard deviation of 4%. Which fund is riskier?
    - A. Fund Y, because its returns are less predictable.
    - B. Fund X, because its returns vary more widely.
    - C. Neither, since both earned the same average return.
    - D. Fund Y, because a lower standard deviation means more risk.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: Standard deviation measures how widely returns spread around the average. Fund X's higher standard deviation means its results are less predictable, even though the average is the same.
    </details>

10. A fund has an average annual return of 12% and a standard deviation of 10%. What is its approximate probable range of returns (one standard deviation)?
    - A. 0% to 12%.
    - B. 12% to 22%.
    - C. 7% to 17%.
    - D. 2% to 22%.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: Probable range = average ± one standard deviation = 12% − 10% to 12% + 10% = 2% to 22%.
    </details>

11. By convention, the overall stock market is assigned a beta of:
    - A. 1.0.
    - B. 0.
    - C. 0.5.
    - D. 100.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: The market's beta is 1.0 by definition. A beta above 1 means a security is more volatile than the market; below 1 means less volatile.
    </details>

12. An equity fund has a beta of 1.30. If the S&P/TSX Composite Index rises by 10%, the fund would be expected to rise by approximately:
    - A. 10%.
    - B. 1.3%.
    - C. 13%.
    - D. 23%.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Expected move = beta × market move = 1.30 × 10% = 13%.
    </details>

13. Which TWO of the following statements about correlation and diversification are correct? (Choose TWO)
    - A. Combining two securities with +1 correlation maximizes the diversification benefit.
    - B. Combining two securities with −1 correlation provides the greatest diversification benefit.
    - C. Adding a second gold mining stock to a portfolio holding one meaningfully diversifies it.
    - D. Systematic risk cannot be eliminated through diversification.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B, D
      Explanation: Perfectly negatively correlated securities offset each other, which gives the most diversification. Perfectly positively correlated ones (such as two gold stocks) give little. Market risk stays no matter how many securities are added.
    </details>

14. The risk that remains in a well-diversified equity portfolio, arising from broad factors like inflation, the business cycle, and interest rates, is known as:
    - A. Market (systematic) risk.
    - B. Firm-specific risk.
    - C. Default risk.
    - D. Liquidity risk.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: Diversification removes firm-specific risk, but market-wide factors affect all securities. That remaining exposure is systematic, or market, risk.
    </details>

15. A portfolio manager's ability to earn returns above what would be expected from the portfolio's beta is measured by:
    - A. Standard deviation.
    - B. Duration.
    - C. Alpha.
    - D. Beta.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Alpha is the excess return beyond what the portfolio's market risk (beta) would predict. A positive alpha suggests the manager added value.
    </details>

16. A manager believes stock prices already reflect all publicly available information, so it isn't possible to consistently find undervalued securities. This manager believes markets are:
    - A. Inefficient.
    - B. Efficient.
    - C. Segmented.
    - D. Illiquid.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: In an efficient market, prices already reflect available information, so trying to beat the market is unlikely to work over time. This belief supports passive management.
    </details>

17. A portfolio manager receives material, non-public information about a company from a personal contact and trades on it before it becomes public. This is:
    - A. A legitimate form of fundamental analysis.
    - B. A form of technical analysis.
    - C. Permitted if disclosed after the trade.
    - D. Illegal insider trading.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: Trading on material, non-public information is illegal insider trading. Disclosing it afterward doesn't make it acceptable.
    </details>

18. A manager who simply buys and holds a broad market index, making no attempt to identify mispriced securities, is practising:
    - A. Passive management.
    - B. Market timing.
    - C. Sector rotation.
    - D. Active security selection.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: Passive managers try to match the market, usually by tracking an index, rather than beat it. Market timing, sector rotation, and security selection are all active strategies.
    </details>

19. The long-term base mix of asset classes (e.g., 60% equities and 40% bonds) that a manager maintains over time, rebalancing as needed, is called the:
    - A. Tactical asset allocation.
    - B. Sector weighting.
    - C. Strategic asset allocation.
    - D. Market timing policy.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: The strategic asset allocation is the long-term policy mix. Tactical allocation refers to short-term shifts away from it to exploit market conditions.
    </details>

20. According to the textbook, the decision that accounts for most of a portfolio's success or failure is:
    - A. Selecting individual securities.
    - B. The asset allocation decision.
    - C. Timing purchases and sales.
    - D. Choosing the fund's benchmark.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: Choosing which asset classes to hold, and in what proportions, has a bigger effect on results than picking individual securities or timing trades.
    </details>

21. An analyst studies a company's debt-to-equity ratio, profit margins, earnings per share, and the economic outlook to estimate a security's true value. This approach is:
    - A. Technical analysis.
    - B. Passive management.
    - C. Market timing.
    - D. Fundamental analysis.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: Fundamental analysis estimates intrinsic value from financial statements, industry conditions, and the economy.
    </details>

22. An analyst ignores financial statements and studies historical price charts and trading volume to predict future price movements. This approach is:
    - A. Technical analysis.
    - B. Fundamental analysis.
    - C. Duration analysis.
    - D. Credit analysis.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: Technical analysts look for patterns in past prices and volume. They assume those patterns repeat and reflect investor psychology.
    </details>

23. A balanced fund manager shifts the fund's asset mix ahead of expected changes in the business cycle. The textbook cautions that this market timing strategy:
    - A. Is reliably profitable for experienced managers.
    - B. Requires no analysis of economic indicators.
    - C. Often mistimes shifts, moving too early or too late.
    - D. Is prohibited for balanced mutual funds.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Market timing depends on calling turning points correctly, and in practice managers often shift their allocation too early or too late.
    </details>

24. Which TWO of the following are legitimate decisions a portfolio manager makes when managing a portfolio? (Choose TWO)
    - A. Guaranteeing a set distribution to unitholders.
    - B. Choosing which sectors to overweight in the equity portion.
    - C. Trading on material non-public information.
    - D. Setting the portfolio's strategic asset mix.
    - E. Tailoring the fund to each unitholder's personal tax situation.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B, D
      Explanation: Sector weighting and the strategic asset mix are core management decisions. Managers can't guarantee distributions, can't legally trade on inside information, and can't tailor a pooled fund to each investor.
    </details>

25. Which sequence correctly orders these securities from lowest to highest on the typical risk/return spectrum?
    - A. T-bills, bonds, preferred shares, common shares, derivatives.
    - B. Bonds, T-bills, common shares, preferred shares, derivatives.
    - C. T-bills, preferred shares, bonds, derivatives, common shares.
    - D. Derivatives, common shares, preferred shares, bonds, T-bills.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: T-bills carry the least risk, followed by bonds, preferred shares, and common shares. Derivatives, being leveraged, sit at the top.
    </details>

26. A bond has a duration of 8. If interest rates rise by 0.5%, the bond's price will change by approximately:
    - A. −8%.
    - B. −0.5%.
    - C. +4%.
    - D. −4%.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: Price change ≈ −duration × change in rates = −8 × 0.5% = −4%. When rates rise, prices fall.
    </details>

27. A client insists on an asset allocation different from the strategic mix recommended by the firm. If the client's KYC information supports the requested allocation, the representative should:
    - A. Refuse to open the account.
    - B. Process it without comment.
    - C. Process it, noting the firm doesn't recommend it.
    - D. Impose the firm's recommended allocation.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: If the request is still suitable, the representative can process it but should make clear, and document, that the firm doesn't recommend that allocation.
    </details>

28. Variance is a statistical measure of:
    - A. The average return of a security.
    - B. How far returns vary from the expected return.
    - C. A bond's sensitivity to interest rate changes.
    - D. How two securities' returns move together.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: Variance measures how far actual returns spread from the mean, and standard deviation is its square root. Option C describes duration and option D describes correlation.
    </details>
