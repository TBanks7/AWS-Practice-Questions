# Chapter 3: Overview of Economics

Original practice questions grounded in the CSI Investment Funds in Canada (IFC) textbook, Chapter 3: microeconomics vs. macroeconomics, GDP measurement, the business cycle, economic indicators, labour market indicators, interest rates, inflation, monetary and fiscal policy, and international economics.

---

1. Which of the following is an example of a MICROeconomic question?
    - A. How a new minimum wage affects hiring in the restaurant industry.
    - B. What causes the national unemployment rate to rise.
    - C. How Bank of Canada rate changes affect overall inflation.
    - D. Why Canada's GDP grew faster than that of the U.S.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: Microeconomics looks at individual markets, firms, and households, such as one industry's hiring. National unemployment, inflation, and GDP are economy-wide questions, so they belong to macroeconomics.
    </details>

2. According to the textbook, the three main groups whose interactions drive economic activity are:
    - A. Banks, regulators, and exchanges.
    - B. Importers, exporters, and central banks.
    - C. Employers, employees, and unions.
    - D. Consumers, firms, and governments.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: The textbook names consumers, firms, and governments as the three groups of economic decision-makers. Their interactions drive economic activity.
    </details>

3. According to the Law of Demand, all else held constant, when the price of a good rises, the quantity demanded will:
    - A. Rise.
    - B. Fall.
    - C. Stay the same.
    - D. Rise at first, then fall.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: Price and quantity demanded move in opposite directions: higher prices mean less is demanded, all else being equal.
    </details>

4. In a laptop market, at a price of $2,000 the quantity demanded exactly equals the quantity supplied. This price is best described as the:
    - A. Bid price.
    - B. Reservation price.
    - C. Equilibrium price.
    - D. Nominal price.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: The equilibrium price is where the demand and supply curves meet — the price at which quantity demanded equals quantity supplied.
    </details>

5. A computer maker buys microchips to build computers that are then sold to consumers. To avoid overstating GDP, national accounts should count:
    - A. Only the value of the finished computer.
    - B. The value of the chip and the computer combined.
    - C. Only the value of the microchip.
    - D. Neither, since both are intermediate goods.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: GDP counts only final goods and services. The chip's value is already included in the computer's price, so counting both would double-count it.
    </details>

6. Under the expenditure approach, GDP is calculated as the sum of:
    - A. Wages, corporate profits, interest, and rental income.
    - B. The value added by each industry in the economy.
    - C. Consumer spending and government spending only.
    - D. Consumption, investment, government spending, and net exports.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: The expenditure approach adds C + I + G + (X − M). Option A describes the income approach and option B the production (value-added) approach.
    </details>

7. The financial press reports that nominal GDP grew 2.4% last year while prices rose 3.1%. What happened to real output?
    - A. It grew by about 5.5%.
    - B. It shrank by about 0.7%.
    - C. It grew by about 2.4%.
    - D. It grew by about 0.7%.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: Real growth ≈ nominal growth − inflation = 2.4% − 3.1% = −0.7%. Prices rose faster than nominal output, so the actual volume of output fell.
    </details>

8. A branch manager notices that labour and product shortages are pushing up wages and inflation, interest rates are rising, and bond prices are falling. Which phase of the business cycle does this describe?
    - A. Trough.
    - B. Recovery.
    - C. Peak.
    - D. Expansion.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: At the peak, the economy is at or above capacity. Shortages push up wages and prices, and rising interest rates drive bond prices down.
    </details>

9. A popular definition holds that an economy is in recession when there has been:
    - A. One quarter of declining real GDP.
    - B. Two consecutive months of declining real GDP.
    - C. Two consecutive quarters of rising unemployment.
    - D. Two consecutive quarters of declining real GDP.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: The popular rule of thumb is two consecutive quarters of declining real GDP. Statistics Canada also looks at the depth, duration, and breadth of a decline.
    </details>

10. Policy makers describe a scenario where growth slows sharply without turning negative, while inflation falls or stays low, as a:
    - A. Soft landing.
    - B. Hard landing.
    - C. Stagflation.
    - D. Trough.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: A soft landing is a slowdown that avoids recession while keeping inflation in check. A hard landing is a slowdown that turns into a recession.
    </details>

11. An economist wants to anticipate where the economy is headed before official GDP figures are released. Which indicator would be most useful?
    - A. The unemployment rate.
    - B. Personal income.
    - C. Housing starts.
    - D. The inflation rate.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Housing starts are a leading indicator, turning before the overall economy. Unemployment and inflation are lagging indicators, and personal income is coincident.
    </details>

12. Which TWO of the following are classified as LAGGING economic indicators? (Choose TWO)
    - A. Housing starts.
    - B. The unemployment rate.
    - C. Stock prices.
    - D. The inflation rate.
    - E. Manufacturers' new orders.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B, D
      Explanation: Unemployment and inflation change only after the economy has already turned, so they are lagging indicators. Housing starts, stock prices, and new orders are leading indicators.
    </details>

13. The participation rate measures:
    - A. The share of the working-age population that is in the labour force.
    - B. The share of the labour force that is unemployed.
    - C. The share of the working-age population that is employed.
    - D. The share of the labour force working full time.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: The participation rate is the labour force (employed plus unemployed job-seekers) as a share of the working-age population. Option B is the unemployment rate and option C is the employment rate.
    </details>

14. A ski resort worker is laid off every spring when the resort closes, then rehired every winter. This is an example of:
    - A. Cyclical unemployment.
    - B. Structural unemployment.
    - C. Seasonal unemployment.
    - D. Frictional unemployment.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Seasonal unemployment comes from predictable seasonal changes in demand for workers, such as ski resorts closing in spring.
    </details>

15. A factory worker whose job was automated lacks the skills needed for available openings and must retrain before finding new work. This illustrates:
    - A. Frictional unemployment.
    - B. Structural unemployment.
    - C. Seasonal unemployment.
    - D. Cyclical unemployment.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: Structural unemployment happens when workers' skills or locations don't match the available jobs, often because of technological change. Frictional unemployment is the short time between jobs for people who are employable.
    </details>

16. A person wants a job but has given up searching and made no job-search effort in the past month. Statistics Canada would classify this person as:
    - A. Frictionally unemployed.
    - B. Structurally unemployed.
    - C. Cyclically unemployed.
    - D. Not part of the labour force.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: Discouraged workers aren't actively looking for work, so they are left out of the labour force entirely and aren't counted as unemployed.
    </details>

17. When the actual unemployment rate falls below the natural unemployment rate, this typically leads to:
    - A. Labour shortages, faster wage growth, and rising inflation.
    - B. Labour surpluses, slower wage growth, and falling inflation.
    - C. A stable economy with no pressure on wages or prices.
    - D. An immediate recession and rising unemployment.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: Unemployment below the natural rate means the labour market is tight. Employers compete for workers, wages rise faster, and inflation tends to pick up.
    </details>

18. If a government's finances deteriorate to the point where investors fear a default, the interest rate it must pay to borrow will typically:
    - A. Fall, as investors seek out the government's bonds.
    - B. Remain unchanged, since governments cannot default.
    - C. Rise, to compensate lenders for greater default risk.
    - D. Fall, since the central bank will lower its policy rate.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Default risk is one of the determinants of interest rates. The more likely a borrower is to default, the higher the rate lenders demand.
    </details>

19. A GIC advertises a nominal rate of 4%, while expected inflation is 1.5%. The approximate real interest rate is:
    - A. 5.5%.
    - B. 2.5%.
    - C. 4.0%.
    - D. 6.0%.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: Real rate ≈ nominal rate − expected inflation = 4% − 1.5% = 2.5%.
    </details>

20. If the CPI rose from 135.4 to 136.6 over a 12-month period, the approximate annual inflation rate was:
    - A. 1.2%.
    - B. 13.6%.
    - C. 0.2%.
    - D. 0.9%.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: Inflation = (136.6 − 135.4) / 135.4 ≈ 0.89%. The 1.2-point rise is a change in index points, not a percentage.
    </details>

21. A sudden spike in global oil prices raises firms' production costs, causing them to raise prices even though consumer demand hasn't changed. This is an example of:
    - A. Demand-pull inflation.
    - B. Disinflation.
    - C. Cost-push inflation.
    - D. Deflation.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Cost-push inflation comes from rising input costs rather than rising demand. Demand-pull inflation happens when demand outruns the economy's capacity to produce.
    </details>

22. The Bank of Canada primarily conducts monetary policy by adjusting its target for the:
    - A. Overnight rate.
    - B. Prime lending rate.
    - C. Five-year mortgage rate.
    - D. 10-year Government of Canada bond yield.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: The Bank of Canada sets a target for the overnight rate, the rate at which major financial institutions lend each other money overnight. Prime and mortgage rates are set by lenders, and bond yields are set by the market.
    </details>

23. To help stimulate a slowing economy, the Bank of Canada lowers the Bank Rate and buys bonds in the open market. This combination is best described as:
    - A. Contractionary monetary policy.
    - B. Expansionary fiscal policy.
    - C. Contractionary fiscal policy.
    - D. Expansionary monetary policy.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: Lowering rates and buying bonds increases the money supply, which is expansionary. Because the central bank is acting rather than the government, it's monetary policy, not fiscal policy.
    </details>

24. Which TWO of the following are tools of fiscal policy, as distinct from monetary policy? (Choose TWO)
    - A. Adjusting the overnight rate target.
    - B. Changing the level of government spending.
    - C. Buying or selling bonds in open-market operations.
    - D. Adjusting the Bank Rate.
    - E. Raising or lowering tax rates.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B, E
      Explanation: Fiscal policy is the government's use of spending and taxation. Setting rates and conducting open-market operations are monetary policy tools used by the Bank of Canada.
    </details>

25. If Canada buys more goods and services from abroad than it sells in a given year, its current account deficit must be financed by:
    - A. A current account surplus in the same year.
    - B. Printing additional currency.
    - C. A surplus on the capital and financial account.
    - D. Higher tariffs on imported goods.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: The balance of payments has to balance. A current account deficit is offset by a capital and financial account surplus, such as selling assets to foreigners or borrowing from abroad.
    </details>
