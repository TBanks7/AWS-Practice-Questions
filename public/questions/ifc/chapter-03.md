# Chapter 3: Overview of Economics

Original practice questions grounded in the CSI Investment Funds in Canada (IFC) textbook, Chapter 3: microeconomics vs. macroeconomics, GDP measurement, the business cycle, economic indicators, labour market indicators, interest rates, inflation, monetary and fiscal policy, and international economics.

---

1. A junior analyst is asked whether the impact of a new minimum wage law on the restaurant industry's hiring is a microeconomic or macroeconomic question. The correct classification is:
    - A. Microeconomics, since it examines the market behaviour of specific firms and workers rather than the economy as a whole.
    - B. Macroeconomics, since wages are always a macroeconomic topic.
    - C. Neither, since minimum wage is a legal issue, not an economic one.
    - D. Both equally, with no meaningful distinction.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
    </details>

2. According to the textbook, the three main groups whose interactions drive economic activity are:
    - A. Banks, regulators, and exchanges.
    - B. Importers, exporters, and central banks.
    - C. Employers, employees, and unions.
    - D. Consumers, firms, and governments.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
    </details>

3. According to the Law of Demand, all else held constant, when the price of a good rises, the quantity demanded will:
    - A. Rise as well.
    - B. Fall.
    - C. Stay exactly the same.
    - D. Become impossible to predict.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
    </details>

4. In a laptop market, at a price of $2,000 the quantity demanded exactly equals the quantity supplied. This price is best described as the:
    - A. Bid price.
    - B. Default premium.
    - C. Equilibrium price.
    - D. Nominal price.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
    </details>

5. A computer manufacturer buys microchips to build computers, which are then sold to consumers. To avoid overstating GDP, national accounts should count:
    - A. Only the market value of the finished computer, since it is the final good.
    - B. The value of both the chip and the computer, added together.
    - C. Only the value of the microchip.
    - D. Neither the chip nor the computer, since both are intermediate goods.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
    </details>

6. Under the expenditure approach, GDP is calculated as the sum of:
    - A. Wages, profits, rents, and interest.
    - B. Exports minus imports only.
    - C. Industry output minus intermediate goods used.
    - D. Personal consumption, investment, government spending, and net exports.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
    </details>

7. The financial press reports nominal GDP grew 2.4% last year while prices rose 3.1%. What actually happened to the economy's real output?
    - A. Real output grew by 5.5%, the sum of both figures.
    - B. Real output actually shrank by approximately 0.7%, since inflation outpaced nominal growth.
    - C. Real output is unaffected by price changes, so it also grew 2.4%.
    - D. There is not enough information to determine real growth.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
    </details>

8. A branch manager notices labour and product shortages are pushing up wages and inflation, interest rates are rising, and bond prices are falling. Which phase of the business cycle does this describe?
    - A. Trough.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>

9. A popular definition holds that an economy is in recession when real GDP declines for:

10. Policy makers describe a scenario where economic growth slows sharply without turning negative, while inflation falls or stays low, as a:
    - A. Soft landing.

11. An economist wants to anticipate where the economy is headed before official GDP figures are released. Which of the following would be most useful, since it tends to peak and trough BEFORE the overall economy?
    - A. The unemployment rate.
    - B. Business loan interest costs.

    - D. The inflation rate, a lagging indicator.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>

    </details>

12. Which TWO of the following are classified as LAGGING economic indicators, changing only after the economy as a whole has already changed? (Choose TWO)
    - A. Housing starts.

13. The participation rate measures:
    - A. The share of the working-age population that is in the labour force.
    - B. The share of the labour force that is unemployed.

    - D. The share of workers employed in seasonal industries.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>

    </details>

14. A ski resort worker is laid off every spring when the resort closes for the off-season, then rehired every winter. This is an example of:
    - A. Cyclical unemployment.
    - B. Structural unemployment.
    - C. Seasonal unemployment.
    - D. Frictional unemployment.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
    </details>

15. A factory worker whose job was automated lacks the technical skills needed for available openings in the local job market and must retrain before finding new work. This illustrates:
    - A. Frictional unemployment.
    - B. Structural unemployment.
    - C. Seasonal unemployment.
    - D. The natural unemployment rate falling to zero.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
    </details>

16. An individual wants a job but has given up actively searching after months of rejection, making no job-search efforts in the past month. Statistics Canada would classify this person as:
    - A. Frictionally unemployed.
    - B. Part of the labour force, actively seeking work.
    - C. Cyclically unemployed.
    - D. A discouraged worker, excluded from the labour force entirely.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
    </details>

17. When the actual unemployment rate falls below the natural unemployment rate, the textbook suggests this typically leads to:
    - A. A shortage of workers, rising wage gains, and higher inflation.
    - B. An automatic decline in inflation.

18. If a central government's finances deteriorate to the point where investors fear it may default on its debt, the interest rate it must pay to borrow will typically:

25. If Canada buys more goods and services from abroad than it sells during a given year, it will run a current account deficit, which must be financed by:
    - A. Printing additional currency.
