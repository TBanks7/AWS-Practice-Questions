# Chapter 14: Understanding Mutual Fund Performance

Original practice questions grounded in the CSI Investment Funds in Canada (IFC) textbook, Chapter 14: measuring mutual fund performance (NAVPS-based return, the Sharpe ratio), benchmark indexes, comparison universes and peer groups, quartile ranking, and survivorship bias.

---

1. A fund's NAVPS was $19.50 at the start of the period and $21.50 at the end, with no distributions, additions, or withdrawals. What was the approximate return?
    - A. 2.00%.
    - B. 10.26%.
    - C. 9.30%.
    - D. 21.50%.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: Return = (ending NAVPS − beginning NAVPS) / beginning NAVPS = ($21.50 − $19.50) / $19.50 ≈ 10.26%. Dividing by the ending value instead gives the wrong answer, 9.30%.
    </details>

2. The standard NAVPS-based method of measuring fund performance generally assumes:
    - A. Distributions paid in cash; no new deposits.
    - B. Distributions reinvested; regular deposits made.
    - C. Distributions paid in cash; withdrawals made.
    - D. Distributions reinvested; no deposits or withdrawals.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: Standard performance figures assume all distributions are reinvested and that no money is added or withdrawn during the period, so the number reflects the fund's performance, not investor cash flows.
    </details>

3. Fund A and Fund B both returned 7% over the same period, but Fund A's standard deviation was 5% and Fund B's was 10%. What does this indicate?
    - A. Fund B took on twice the risk for the same return.
    - B. The two funds are identical in every respect.
    - C. Fund A took on more risk than Fund B.
    - D. Standard deviation doesn't matter here.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: Same return, double the volatility: Fund B had a worse risk-adjusted result. That's why return alone isn't enough to compare funds.
    </details>

4. The Sharpe ratio is calculated as:
    - A. (Return − risk-free rate) ÷ beta.
    - B. Standard deviation ÷ (return − risk-free rate).
    - C. (Return − risk-free rate) ÷ standard deviation.
    - D. (Return − benchmark return) ÷ tracking error.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: The Sharpe ratio measures excess return over the risk-free rate per unit of total risk (standard deviation). Higher is better.
    </details>

5. A Canadian equity fund had an average return of 6%, a standard deviation of 5%, and the risk-free rate averaged 1%. What is its Sharpe ratio?
    - A. 0.2.
    - B. 1.0.
    - C. 1.2.
    - D. 5.0.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: Sharpe ratio = (6% − 1%) / 5% = 1.0. Leaving out the risk-free rate (6 / 5) gives 1.2, which is wrong.
    </details>

6. In the Sharpe ratio, the risk-free rate is typically approximated using:
    - A. The S&P/TSX Composite Index return.
    - B. The fund's management expense ratio.
    - C. The prime lending rate.
    - D. The average three-month T-bill rate.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: Government of Canada T-bills are treated as essentially risk-free, so their average rate over the period is used as the risk-free rate.
    </details>

7. The most suitable benchmark for a Canadian bond fund would be:
    - A. The FTSE Canada Universe Bond Index.
    - B. The S&P/TSX Composite Index.
    - C. The S&P 500.
    - D. The FTSE Canada 91-Day T-Bill Index.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: A benchmark should match the fund's asset class. The Universe Bond Index covers the Canadian bond market; the T-bill index fits money market funds.
    </details>

8. Which TWO of the following are standards a sound performance measure should meet? (Choose TWO)
    - A. It should favour the fund manager.
    - B. It should be easy for the fund company to adjust.
    - C. It should identify superior, ordinary, and inferior performance.
    - D. It should ignore the length of the evaluation period.
    - E. It should be realistic enough to be useful.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C, E
      Explanation: A good performance measure separates superior, ordinary, and inferior performance, is realistic enough to use, and can't easily be manipulated.
    </details>

9. Which benchmark would be most appropriate for evaluating a U.S. equity fund?
    - A. The MSCI EAFE Index.
    - B. The S&P 500.
    - C. The S&P/TSX 60.
    - D. The MSCI World Index.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: The S&P 500 represents large U.S. companies. EAFE covers Europe, Australasia, and the Far East, the S&P/TSX 60 covers Canada, and MSCI World is global.
    </details>

10. A representative compares a Canadian bond fund's return with the S&P/TSX Composite Index. Why is this comparison flawed?
    - A. Bond funds always outperform equity indexes.
    - B. The S&P/TSX tracks only 60 companies.
    - C. Bond funds and equity indexes have the same risk.
    - D. The benchmark doesn't match the fund's asset class.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: A benchmark only makes sense if it matches the fund's asset class. Comparing a bond fund to an equity index compares different risks and securities.
    </details>

11. A collection of funds with similar mandates, used to compare a specific fund's relative performance, is called a:
    - A. A comparison universe.
    - B. A benchmark index.
    - C. A quartile.
    - D. A style box.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: A comparison universe groups funds with similar mandates so a fund can be ranked against its peers.
    </details>

12. A fund returned 12% over one year while its peer group averaged 9%. The fund:
    - A. Underperformed its peer group.
    - B. Matched its peer group.
    - C. Outperformed its peer group.
    - D. Beat its benchmark index.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: The fund beat the peer group average by 3 percentage points. We can't tell from this whether it beat its benchmark index, which is a separate comparison.
    </details>

13. Quartile ranking divides a peer group's performance into how many equal parts?
    - A. Two.
    - B. Four.
    - C. Five.
    - D. Ten.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: Quartiles split the peer group into four equal parts, each holding 25% of the funds.
    </details>

14. A fund in the first quartile of its peer group for a period achieved:
    - A. Returns in the bottom 25%.
    - B. Returns in the middle 50%.
    - C. Exactly the median return.
    - D. Returns in the top 25%.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: The first quartile is the top 25% of performers, and the fourth quartile is the bottom 25%.
    </details>

15. Fund X ranks in the first quartile some years and the fourth in others, while Fund Y ranks in the second quartile every year. For suitability purposes, the representative should generally view:
    - A. Fund Y's consistency as more valuable.
    - B. Fund X's first-quartile years as superior.
    - C. Quartile rankings as irrelevant.
    - D. Both funds as equally suitable.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: Consistent above-median results are generally worth more to a client than erratic swings between the top and bottom of the peer group.
    </details>

16. A comparison universe tends to look better over longer periods partly because:
    - A. Terminated funds remain in the universe.
    - B. Only newly launched funds are included.
    - C. Weak funds that close drop out of the universe.
    - D. Bond funds are removed over time.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: This is survivorship bias. Poor performers are closed or merged away, so only the survivors remain and the historical record looks better than it really was.
    </details>

17. A comparison universe that is defined too broadly may fail to properly assess risk because:
    - A. All funds in a universe carry the same risk.
    - B. Specialized managers may differ in risk from the universe.
    - C. Broad universes eliminate survivorship bias.
    - D. Risk isn't relevant to fund comparisons.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: A manager focused on, say, small caps or high-yield bonds may carry very different risk from the broad universe, so ranking them together can be misleading.
    </details>

18. Firms such as Morningstar, which collect performance data from many funds and build comparison universes, are known as:
    - A. Self-regulatory organizations.
    - B. Custodians.
    - C. Underwriters.
    - D. Appraisal firms.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: Appraisal firms collect and publish fund performance data and rankings, which representatives and investors use to compare funds.
    </details>

19. Which TWO of the following are measures the textbook identifies for quantifying a fund's volatility? (Choose TWO)
    - A. Standard deviation.
    - B. Assets under management.
    - C. Beta.
    - D. Sales commission.
    - E. Number of unitholders.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A, C
      Explanation: Standard deviation measures total volatility, and beta measures volatility relative to the market or benchmark.
    </details>

20. A fund has earned close to 5% every year for 20 years, with very little variation, giving it a low standard deviation. This suggests:
    - A. The fund is likely to lose money next year.
    - B. Future returns may be fairly consistent.
    - C. Its returns are completely unpredictable.
    - D. Its beta is far above 1.0.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: A low standard deviation means past returns were consistent, which makes future returns more predictable — but past performance never guarantees future results.
    </details>

21. A fund's beta measures:
    - A. The fund's total return over its life.
    - B. The fund's management expense ratio.
    - C. How long the fund has existed.
    - D. Its volatility relative to its market.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: Beta shows how much a fund moves compared with its market or benchmark. A beta above 1 means more volatile; below 1 means less.
    </details>

22. When comparing fund performance, investors should focus mainly on longer-term results because they:
    - A. Show performance across a full market cycle.
    - B. Always predict short-term results.
    - C. Are required by regulators.
    - D. Are always higher than one-year results.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: Longer periods show how a manager handles every phase of the business cycle, so they're a better test of skill than one good or bad year.
    </details>

23. In the case study, the ABC Emerging Markets Equity Fund posted a negative return, yet Tom concluded it was performing well. This was mainly because the fund:
    - A. Had the highest absolute return in the portfolio.
    - B. Had a beta above 1.5.
    - C. Beat most of its peers and its benchmark.
    - D. Charged the lowest fee among its peers.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Relative performance matters. The fund outperformed about 90% of its peer group and lost less than its benchmark in a falling market.
    </details>

24. In the case study, the ABC Emerging Markets Equity Fund had a beta of 0.8 relative to its benchmark. The fund was:
    - A. More volatile than its benchmark.
    - B. Less volatile than its benchmark.
    - C. Exactly as volatile as its benchmark.
    - D. Uncorrelated with its benchmark.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: A beta below 1.0 means the fund tends to move less than its benchmark — about 80% as much.
    </details>

25. A formal document that guides the overall management of a portfolio, and against which a manager's performance can be measured, is a(n):
    - A. Fund Facts document.
    - B. Prospectus.
    - C. Comparison universe.
    - D. Investment policy statement.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: An investment policy statement sets out the portfolio's objectives, constraints, and guidelines, which gives a standard to measure the manager against.
    </details>

26. An investor puts 10% of their portfolio into a fund classified as a gold fund, but the fund is currently holding 50% of its assets in cash. This illustrates that:
    - A. A fund's category may not reflect its current holdings.
    - B. Gold funds must hold 100% gold at all times.
    - C. Holding cash in a fund is prohibited.
    - D. The manager has broken securities law.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: Managers have discretion, so a fund's actual holdings may differ from what its category suggests. That can throw off an investor's intended asset allocation.
    </details>

27. When comparing a fund's return with its benchmark, it is essential to:
    - A. Use different periods for a broader view.
    - B. Ignore the benchmark's past returns.
    - C. Use the same time period for both.
    - D. Compare returns over a single day.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: The fund and its benchmark must be measured over exactly the same period, or the comparison doesn't mean anything.
    </details>

28. According to the textbook, the most popular method of relative performance evaluation compares a fund's return with:
    - A. A single competitor fund.
    - B. A peer group of similar funds.
    - C. The fund company's share price.
    - D. The Bank of Canada's policy rate.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: Comparing a fund against a peer group of funds with similar mandates is the most widely used way to evaluate relative performance.
    </details>
