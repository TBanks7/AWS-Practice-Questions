# Chapter 14: Understanding Mutual Fund Performance

Original practice questions grounded in the CSI Investment Funds in Canada (IFC) textbook, Chapter 14: measuring mutual fund performance (NAVPS-based return, the Sharpe ratio), benchmark indexes, comparison universes and peer groups, quartile ranking, and survivorship bias.

---

1. A mutual fund's NAVPS was $19.50 at the beginning of the period and $21.50 at the end, with no additions or withdrawals during the period. What is the approximate percentage gain?
    - A. 2.00%.
    - B. 10.26% [($21.50 - $19.50) / $19.50].
    - C. 21.50%.
    - D. 9.30%.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
    </details>

2. The standard NAVPS-based method of measuring mutual fund performance generally assumes:
    - A. The investor made large withdrawals throughout the period.
    - B. Dividends are never reinvested.
    - C. The client added new money partway through the period.
    - D. All dividends are reinvested and no additions or withdrawals occurred during the period.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
    </details>

3. Fund A and Fund B both earned a 7% return over the same period, but Fund A had a standard deviation of 5% while Fund B had a standard deviation of 10%. What does this indicate?
    - A. Fund B took on twice the risk of Fund A to achieve the identical return.
    - B. Fund A and Fund B are identical in every respect.
    - C. Fund A is clearly a worse investment than Fund B.
    - D. Standard deviation has no bearing on comparing the two funds.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
    </details>

4. The Sharpe ratio is calculated as:
    - A. The portfolio's standard deviation divided by its total return.
    - B. The portfolio's beta multiplied by the risk-free rate.
    - C. The portfolio's return minus the risk-free rate, divided by the portfolio's standard deviation.
    - D. The portfolio's alpha divided by its tracking error.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
    </details>

5. A Canadian equity fund had an average return of 6%, a standard deviation of 5%, and the risk-free rate averaged 1% over the period. What is the fund's Sharpe ratio?
    - A. 0.2.
    - B. 1.0 [(6-1)/5].
    - C. 5.0.
    - D. 6.0.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
    </details>

6. In the Sharpe ratio formula, the risk-free rate is typically approximated using:
    - A. The S&P/TSX Composite Index return.

16. A comparison universe tends to look increasingly favourable over longer time periods partly because:
    - A. All funds, including terminated ones, remain in the universe forever.
