# Chapter 7: Types of Investment Products and How They Are Traded

Original practice questions grounded in the CSI Investment Funds in Canada (IFC) textbook, Chapter 7: fixed-income securities, bond pricing and yield, common and preferred shares, underwriting and the prospectus, market transactions, and derivative securities.

---

1. Fixed-income securities such as bonds represent:
    - A. Equity ownership in the issuing company.
    - B. A residual claim on company profits after all other obligations.
    - C. A contract giving the holder voting rights.
    - D. Debt of the issuing entity, making the investor a creditor entitled to regular coupon payments and repayment of principal at maturity.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
    </details>

2. An investor holds a $10,000 bond with an 8% annual coupon rate, paid semi-annually. What is each semi-annual coupon payment?
    - A. $400 (8% x $10,000 / 2).
    - B. $800.
    - C. $80.
    - D. $10,800.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
    </details>

3. A $10,000 bond is currently quoted at a price of 96. What does this indicate, and what would it cost to purchase?
    - A. The bond is trading at a premium and would cost $10,600.
    - B. The bond is trading at par and would cost $10,000.
    - C. The bond is trading at a discount and would cost $9,600.
    - D. The bond is trading at a premium and would cost $9,600.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
    </details>

4. Among Government of Canada bonds, provincial bonds, and municipal bonds, credit rating agencies generally rank default risk from lowest to highest as:
    - A. Municipal, provincial, federal.
    - B. Federal, provincial, municipal.
    - C. Provincial, federal, municipal.
    - D. All three carry identical default risk.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
    </details>

5. Treasury bills differ from most bonds in that T-bills:
    - A. Pay semi-annual coupon interest like a standard bond.
    - B. Can only be purchased by retail investors, never institutions.
    - C. Are taxed as capital gains rather than income.
    - D. Pay no stated interest; instead, they are sold at a discount and mature at par, with the difference taxed as income.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>

6. A municipality issues a $1 million debenture structured so that $100,000 becomes due each year over a 10-year period. This structure is known as a(n):
    - A. Instalment (serial) debenture.

    - D. Bankers' acceptance.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>

7. A corporate bond backed by a specific pledged asset that bondholders can claim if the company defaults is called a:
    - A. Debenture.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>

8. A corporation is most likely to call (redeem early) its outstanding bonds when:
    - A. Interest rates have risen well above the bond's coupon rate.
    - B. Interest rates have fallen below the bond's coupon rate, allowing refinancing at a lower cost.
    - C. The bond is about to reach its natural maturity date anyway.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>

    </details>

9. When the underlying company's share price rises significantly, the value of its convertible bonds will tend to:
    - A. Remain completely fixed, unaffected by share price.
    - B. Fall, since bonds and shares move in opposite directions.
    - C. Only be affected by changes in interest rates.

    </details>

10. A client purchases a 7-year non-redeemable GIC. Regarding CDIC deposit insurance, the representative should note that:
    - A. CDIC does not cover GICs with terms of more than five years.
    - B. All GICs are automatically covered regardless of term.
    - C. Only redeemable GICs are ever eligible for CDIC coverage.

11. A "laddered" GIC strategy, which divides an investment into several different terms (e.g., one through five years), is primarily designed to:
    - A. Maximize the interest rate by locking the entire amount into the longest term.
    - B. Guarantee a fixed dividend yield.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>

    </details>

12. A banker's acceptance is best described as:
    - A. Common shares issued by a chartered bank.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>

13. If general interest rates in the economy rise from 3% to 4%, existing fixed-income mutual funds holding older, lower-coupon bonds will generally:
    - A. Increase in value, since higher rates make bonds more attractive.
    - B. Be completely unaffected, since bond funds have no interest rate exposure.
    - C. Only be affected if the fund holds equities as well.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
    </details>

14. Comparing two bond funds with identical credit quality but different average maturities, for the same change in yields, the fund holding longer-term bonds will generally experience:
    - A. Greater price volatility than the fund holding shorter-term bonds.
    - B. Identical price volatility to the shorter-term fund.
    - C. Less price volatility than the shorter-term fund.
    - D. No price change at all, regardless of maturity.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>

    </details>

15. A 4-year, 9% bond is currently trading at a price of 96.77. Which TWO of the following statements about this bond's current yield are correct? (Choose TWO)
    - A. Current yield accounts for the capital gain the investor will realize at maturity.
    - B. Current yield is calculated as annual coupon income divided by the current market price.
    - C. Current yield and yield to maturity will always be identical for any bond.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>

    </details>

16. Under normal market conditions, the yield curve typically slopes upward because:
    - A. Short-term bonds always carry more default risk than long-term bonds.
    - B. Regulators require long-term bonds to pay a fixed minimum premium.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>

    </details>

17. Unlike a bond's coupon payment, a common share's dividend is:
