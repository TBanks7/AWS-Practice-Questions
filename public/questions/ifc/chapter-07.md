# Chapter 7: Types of Investment Products and How They Are Traded

Original practice questions grounded in the CSI Investment Funds in Canada (IFC) textbook, Chapter 7: fixed-income securities, bond pricing and yield, common and preferred shares, underwriting and the prospectus, market transactions, and derivative securities.

---

1. Fixed-income securities such as bonds represent:
    - A. An ownership stake in the issuing company.
    - B. A residual claim on profits after all obligations.
    - C. A right to vote at shareholder meetings.
    - D. A loan to the issuer, making the investor a creditor.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: A bond is debt. The investor lends money to the issuer and is entitled to coupon payments and repayment of principal at maturity. Ownership, residual claims, and votes belong to shareholders.
    </details>

2. An investor holds a $10,000 bond with an 8% annual coupon rate, paid semi-annually. What is each semi-annual coupon payment?
    - A. $400.
    - B. $800.
    - C. $80.
    - D. $40.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: Annual interest is 8% × $10,000 = $800, paid in two instalments of $400.
    </details>

3. A $10,000 bond is currently quoted at a price of 96. The bond is trading:
    - A. At a premium, costing $10,600.
    - B. At par, costing $10,000.
    - C. At a discount, costing $9,600.
    - D. At a premium, costing $9,600.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Bond prices are quoted per $100 of face value. A price of 96 means 96% of par, so the bond costs $9,600. Anything below par is a discount.
    </details>

4. Among Government of Canada, provincial, and municipal bonds, credit rating agencies generally rank default risk from lowest to highest as:
    - A. Municipal, provincial, federal.
    - B. Federal, provincial, municipal.
    - C. Provincial, federal, municipal.
    - D. Federal, municipal, provincial.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: The federal government has the broadest taxing power and the lowest default risk. Provinces come next, and municipalities, with the narrowest tax base, carry the most risk of the three.
    </details>

5. Treasury bills differ from most bonds in that T-bills:
    - A. Pay semi-annual coupon interest.
    - B. Are issued with terms of 5 to 10 years.
    - C. Are taxed as capital gains at maturity.
    - D. Are sold at a discount and mature at par.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: T-bills pay no coupon. The investor's return is the difference between the discounted purchase price and par, and that difference is taxed as interest income.
    </details>

6. A municipality issues a $1 million debenture structured so that $100,000 becomes due each year over 10 years. This structure is known as a(n):
    - A. Instalment (serial) debenture.
    - B. Convertible debenture.
    - C. Sinking fund bond.
    - D. Perpetual bond.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: A serial debenture matures in instalments, with part of the issue coming due each year. That matches repayment to the life of the asset being financed.
    </details>

7. A corporate bond backed by a specific pledged asset that bondholders can claim if the company defaults is called a:
    - A. Debenture.
    - B. Treasury bill.
    - C. Secured bond.
    - D. Convertible bond.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: A secured bond is backed by specific collateral. A debenture is unsecured and relies only on the issuer's general credit.
    </details>

8. A corporation is most likely to call (redeem early) its outstanding bonds when:
    - A. Rates have risen well above the coupon rate.
    - B. Rates have fallen below the coupon rate.
    - C. The bond is close to its maturity date.
    - D. The issuer's credit rating has been downgraded.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: When rates fall, the issuer can call the higher-coupon bonds and refinance more cheaply. That's why a call feature works against the bondholder.
    </details>

9. When the underlying company's share price rises significantly, the value of its convertible bonds will tend to:
    - A. Remain unchanged, since bond values are fixed.
    - B. Fall, since bonds and shares move inversely.
    - C. Change only in response to interest rates.
    - D. Rise, reflecting the value of the conversion feature.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: A convertible bond can be exchanged for shares, so as the share price rises, the bond's value becomes increasingly tied to the value of those shares.
    </details>

10. Compared with a redeemable GIC of the same term, a non-redeemable GIC typically offers:
    - A. A higher interest rate in exchange for less liquidity.
    - B. A lower interest rate in exchange for more liquidity.
    - C. The same rate, but without deposit insurance.
    - D. Returns linked to a stock market index.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: Locking funds in until maturity earns a higher rate. Redeemable GICs pay less in exchange for the flexibility to cash out early.
    </details>

11. A "laddered" GIC strategy, which spreads an investment across several terms (e.g., one through five years), is primarily designed to:
    - A. Lock in the highest rate by using the longest term.
    - B. Guarantee a return linked to equity markets.
    - C. Reduce interest rate risk by staggering maturities.
    - D. Eliminate the need to ever reinvest maturing funds.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: With staggered maturities, part of the money comes due each year and can be reinvested at current rates. That avoids locking everything in at one rate.
    </details>

12. A banker's acceptance is best described as:
    - A. Common shares issued by a chartered bank.
    - B. A bank-guaranteed commercial draft sold at a discount.
    - C. A long-term bond issued by a chartered bank.
    - D. A deposit insured by the CDIC.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: A BA is a short-term commercial draft guaranteed by the borrower's bank. Like a T-bill, it is sold at a discount and matures at face value, usually at a slightly higher yield.
    </details>

13. If general interest rates rise from 3% to 4%, fixed-income funds holding older, lower-coupon bonds will generally:
    - A. Increase in value.
    - B. Remain unchanged.
    - C. Change only if they also hold equities.
    - D. Decrease in value.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: Bond prices move inversely to interest rates. When new bonds pay 4%, existing lower-coupon bonds are worth less, so the fund's value falls.
    </details>

14. Comparing two bond funds with identical credit quality but different average maturities, for the same change in yields, the fund holding longer-term bonds will generally experience:
    - A. Greater price volatility.
    - B. Identical price volatility.
    - C. Less price volatility.
    - D. No price change.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: Longer-term bonds are more sensitive to interest rate changes, so their prices move more for the same change in yield.
    </details>

15. A 4-year, 9% bond is trading at a price of 96.77. Which TWO statements about its current yield are correct? (Choose TWO)
    - A. Current yield includes the gain the investor will realize at maturity.
    - B. Current yield is annual coupon income divided by the market price.
    - C. Current yield and yield to maturity are always identical.
    - D. This bond's current yield is above its 9% coupon rate.
    - E. This bond's current yield is below its 9% coupon rate.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B, D
      Explanation: Current yield = 9 / 96.77 ≈ 9.30%, which is above the coupon because the bond trades below par. Current yield ignores the gain at maturity; yield to maturity includes it.
    </details>

16. Under normal market conditions, the yield curve slopes upward because:
    - A. Short-term bonds carry more default risk than long-term bonds.
    - B. Regulators require a minimum premium on long-term bonds.
    - C. Investors demand more yield for longer-term uncertainty.
    - D. Long-term bonds receive more favourable tax treatment.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Longer maturities carry more uncertainty and more interest rate risk, so investors normally require higher yields to hold them.
    </details>

17. Unlike a bond's coupon payment, a common share dividend is:
    - A. Paid at the discretion of the board of directors.
    - B. A contractual obligation of the company.
    - C. Fixed for the life of the share at issue.
    - D. Guaranteed once it has been paid in a prior year.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: The board decides whether to pay a common dividend and how much. A history of paying dividends doesn't guarantee future payments.
    </details>

18. A preferred share has a $25 par value and pays an annual dividend of $1.50. What is its dividend yield at par?
    - A. 1.5%.
    - B. 25%.
    - C. 16.7%.
    - D. 6%.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: Dividend yield = annual dividend / price = $1.50 / $25 = 6%. Option C comes from dividing the wrong way ($25 / $1.50).
    </details>

19. A company skips its dividend for two years on a cumulative preferred share. Before paying any common dividends, the company must first pay:
    - A. Nothing further; skipped dividends are forfeited.
    - B. All accumulated dividend arrears.
    - C. Half of the missed dividends.
    - D. Interest on the missed dividends only.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: Unpaid cumulative preferred dividends build up as arrears, and all of them must be paid before common shareholders get anything. With non-cumulative shares, skipped dividends are lost.
    </details>

20. A retraction feature lets preferred shareholders force the company to redeem their shares. If the company may pay in either cash or common shares, this is a:
    - A. Hard retraction.
    - B. Cumulative feature.
    - C. Soft retraction.
    - D. Call feature.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: In a soft retraction, the company can pay in cash or common shares. A hard retraction must be paid in cash. A call feature is the company's right to redeem, not the shareholder's.
    </details>

21. In a company bankruptcy, preferred shareholders generally rank:
    - A. Ahead of common shareholders, behind creditors.
    - B. Ahead of both common shareholders and creditors.
    - C. Behind common shareholders, ahead of creditors.
    - D. Equally with secured bondholders.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: Creditors, including bondholders, are paid first, then preferred shareholders, then common shareholders, who have the residual claim.
    </details>

22. The document that provides a complete description of an issuing company and the securities being offered to the public is the:
    - A. Fund Facts document.
    - B. Annual report.
    - C. Offering memorandum.
    - D. Prospectus.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: A public offering needs a prospectus that fully discloses the issuer and the securities. An offering memorandum is used for exempt (private) distributions.
    </details>

23. If a company's prospectus omits a material fact that would likely have influenced an investor's decision, the issuing corporation is:
    - A. Shielded, since the regulator approved the document.
    - B. Legally liable for the omission.
    - C. Liable only if the omission was intentional.
    - D. Liable only if the investor sold at a loss.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: The issuer is liable for misrepresentations in its prospectus, including leaving out material facts. A regulator's receipt for the prospectus doesn't shift that responsibility.
    </details>

24. A large, well-known company that issues securities frequently may use a simplified prospectus process known as:
    - A. A bought deal.
    - B. A private placement.
    - C. Shelf registration.
    - D. A best-efforts offering.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Shelf registration lets frequent, well-known issuers file one base prospectus and issue securities from it over time without a full new prospectus each time.
    </details>

25. An investor tells their broker, "Buy 100 shares of XYZ right now at whatever the current price is." This is a:
    - A. Market order.
    - B. Limit order.
    - C. Stop-loss order.
    - D. Good-till-cancelled order.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: A market order is filled immediately at the best available price. A limit order sets a maximum buying price or a minimum selling price.
    </details>

26. A client wants to buy securities using money partly borrowed from their brokerage firm, paying interest on the loan. This requires a:
    - A. Cash account.
    - B. Discretionary account.
    - C. RESP.
    - D. Margin account.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: A margin account lets the investor borrow part of the purchase price from the dealer, which adds leverage and risk. In a cash account, trades must be paid for in full.
    </details>

27. Which TWO of the following correctly describe short selling? (Choose TWO)
    - A. The investor first buys the security, then sells it later.
    - B. The investor sells a security they don't own.
    - C. The investor profits if the price falls.
    - D. Mutual funds may sell short without restriction.
    - E. The investor's maximum loss is limited to the sale proceeds.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B, C
      Explanation: A short seller sells borrowed securities hoping to buy them back cheaper. Because the price can rise without limit, the potential loss is theoretically unlimited.
    </details>

28. National Instrument 81-102 generally caps a conventional mutual fund's use of derivatives, as a percentage of net assets, at:
    - A. 10%.
    - B. 20%.
    - C. 25%.
    - D. 50%.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: NI 81-102 generally limits conventional mutual funds to 10% of net assets in derivatives (used for non-hedging purposes). Alternative mutual funds have more room.
    </details>
