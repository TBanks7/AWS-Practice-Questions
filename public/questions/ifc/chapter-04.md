# Chapter 4: Getting to Know the Client

Original practice questions grounded in the CSI Investment Funds in Canada (IFC) textbook, Chapter 4: the financial planning process, KYC information requirements, investment objectives, net worth, risk profile (tolerance vs. capacity), the life-cycle hypothesis, and the financial planning pyramid.

---

1. Which of the following correctly lists the steps of the financial planning process, in order?
    - A. Collect data, establish the relationship, analyze data, recommend, implement, review.
    - B. Establish the relationship, collect data, analyze data, recommend, implement, review.
    - C. Establish the relationship, analyze data, collect data, recommend, review, implement.
    - D. Collect data, analyze data, establish the relationship, implement, recommend, review.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: The six steps are: establish the client-advisor relationship, collect data, analyze data, recommend strategies, implement the recommendations, and conduct periodic reviews.
    </details>

2. According to the textbook, a financial plan must, among other things:
    - A. Guarantee a minimum rate of return.
    - B. Target the highest achievable return.
    - C. Remain fixed once agreed with the client.
    - D. Be achievable and flexible as circumstances change.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: A plan must be achievable and able to adapt to changes in lifestyle and income. Plans are reviewed and revised over time, and they never guarantee a return.
    </details>

3. At a minimum, when opening an account for a new client, the KYC rule requires the representative to gather all of the following EXCEPT:
    - A. Investment needs and objectives.
    - B. Risk profile and investment time horizon.
    - C. Copies of the client's recent tax returns.
    - D. Financial circumstances, such as income and net worth.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: KYC requires information about the client's needs and objectives, financial and personal circumstances, investment knowledge, risk profile, and time horizon. Tax returns are not a minimum requirement.
    </details>

4. A client's order form indicates "safety of capital" as their primary objective. According to the textbook, which fund type is the ONLY one that meets this objective?
    - A. A money market fund.
    - B. A short-term bond fund.
    - C. A mortgage fund.
    - D. A balanced fund.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: Only money market funds meet the safety-of-capital objective. Short-term bond funds and mortgage funds are conservative, but their unit values still fluctuate with interest rates.
    </details>

5. A client seeking "current income" from mortgage and bond funds should generally be prepared to accept:
    - A. No fluctuation in the value of their investment.
    - B. A shorter time horizon than for a money market fund.
    - C. The same capital stability as a money market fund.
    - D. A longer time horizon than for a money market fund.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: Mortgage and bond funds are more volatile than money market funds, so an investor seeking current income needs a longer time horizon to ride out fluctuations in value.
    </details>

6. A client wants their portfolio to grow significantly in value over time and is willing to accept volatility to achieve this. This objective is best described as:
    - A. Safety of capital.
    - B. Capital growth.
    - C. Current income.
    - D. Liquidity.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: Capital growth means increasing the value of the investment over time, usually through equities, and it comes with more volatility.
    </details>

7. A 40-year-old with two teenagers approaching university has very different short-term goals than a single 25-year-old with no dependants. This illustrates the impact of:
    - A. Investment knowledge.
    - B. Risk capacity.
    - C. Personal circumstances.
    - D. Investment time horizon only.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Personal circumstances such as age, marital status, and dependants shape a client's goals and obligations. They are a separate KYC element from financial circumstances.
    </details>

8. The amount of money left over after paying for all household bills and living expenses, available for saving, is called:
    - A. Discretionary income.
    - B. Net worth.
    - C. Disposable income.
    - D. Gross income.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: Discretionary income is what remains after all bills and living expenses. Disposable income is income after taxes but before living expenses.
    </details>

9. A client owns a home worth $400,000 and $50,000 in investments, and owes $250,000 on the mortgage plus $10,000 in other debt. What is the client's net worth?
    - A. $450,000.
    - B. $650,000.
    - C. $260,000.
    - D. $190,000.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: Net worth = total assets − total liabilities = ($400,000 + $50,000) − ($250,000 + $10,000) = $190,000.
    </details>

10. A client's net worth rose sharply this year mainly because their home's market value increased. Why should the representative be cautious about relying on this figure alone?
    - A. Home values may not be included in a net worth statement.
    - B. Net worth should be calculated using the home's purchase price.
    - C. The home is illiquid, so the gain doesn't add accessible savings.
    - D. Rising home values mean the client should invest more aggressively.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Real estate is included in net worth at market value, but it isn't easy to turn into cash. A higher home value doesn't give the client more savings to invest or use in an emergency.
    </details>

11. A client is emotionally comfortable with significant volatility (high risk tolerance) but has little savings and an unstable income (low risk capacity). Under NI 31-103, the client's overall risk profile should reflect:
    - A. The higher of the two measures.
    - B. The lower of the two measures.
    - C. An average of the two measures.
    - D. Risk tolerance only.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: NI 31-103 guidance says the risk profile should reflect the lower of risk tolerance (willingness) and risk capacity (ability to absorb losses).
    </details>

12. Why does the textbook suggest that more knowledgeable investors are generally better able to bear investment risk?
    - A. They better understand the risk-return trade-off.
    - B. They are exempt from certain KYC requirements.
    - C. They are more likely to earn above-average returns.
    - D. They typically have higher incomes and net worth.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: Knowledgeable investors understand how risk and return relate and how that should be reflected in their portfolio, so they are less likely to react badly to volatility. Knowledge doesn't exempt anyone from KYC or guarantee better returns.
    </details>

13. Which TWO of the following distinguish a typical Stage 2 (family commitment years) client from a Stage 1 (early earning years) client? (Choose TWO)
    - A. Stage 2 clients generally have more liquidity than Stage 1 clients.
    - B. Stage 2 clients are usually more risk tolerant than Stage 1 clients.
    - C. Stage 2 clients generally carry heavier commitments, such as a mortgage and childcare.
    - D. Stage 1 clients typically need life insurance more urgently than Stage 2 clients.
    - E. Life insurance tends to become a requirement rather than a discretionary expense.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C, E
      Explanation: Stage 2 brings family obligations such as a mortgage and childcare, which strain liquidity and make life insurance a necessity rather than an option.
    </details>

14. Clients in Stage 4 (nearing retirement) typically differ from Stage 3 clients in that Stage 4 clients:
    - A. Have heavier family and mortgage commitments.
    - B. Are generally more willing to take on risk.
    - C. Have a longer horizon before needing savings.
    - D. Have fewer family commitments and are more risk averse.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: By Stage 4, children are usually independent. Clients become more risk averse because they will soon depend on their savings.
    </details>

15. As a client moves into Stage 5 (retired), their asset allocation will most likely shift toward:
    - A. A larger equity component to extend growth.
    - B. A smaller equity component and more fixed income.
    - C. An allocation identical to the one held in Stage 4.
    - D. Mostly growth funds to offset a longer lifespan.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: In retirement, the focus moves to income and preserving capital, so the equity share typically falls in favour of fixed income and safer investments.
    </details>

16. A 62-year-old client has an unusually high tolerance for risk, inconsistent with the typical Stage 5 profile. The representative should:
    - A. Treat the life-cycle model as a guide and tailor to the client's actual profile.
    - B. Apply the typical Stage 5 allocation regardless of the client's profile.
    - C. Decline to serve the client until their risk tolerance declines.
    - D. Assume the stated risk tolerance is inaccurate and lower it.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: The life-cycle hypothesis describes typical clients, and not every client fits it. Recommendations should reflect the client's actual KYC profile, including both risk tolerance and risk capacity.
    </details>

17. In the financial planning pyramid, which of the following belongs at the base ("Security") level?
    - A. Precious metals and IPOs.
    - B. Stocks and mutual funds.
    - C. Insurance and a will.
    - D. Bonds and GICs.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: The base (Security) is insurance and a will. Next comes Independence (paying off debt, a home, an RRSP, an emergency fund), followed by the investment tiers from conservative to very aggressive.
    </details>

18. Art, IPOs, over-the-counter securities, real estate, and precious metals sit at which level of the financial planning pyramid?
    - A. Security.
    - B. Independence.
    - C. Conservative investment.
    - D. Very aggressive investment.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: These highly speculative holdings sit at the top of the pyramid, in the very aggressive tier.
    </details>

19. A client wants to invest in precious metals but has no will and no life insurance in place. What does the financial planning pyramid suggest?
    - A. This is acceptable, since each tier is independent of the others.
    - B. The foundation is missing, so security and independence should come first.
    - C. Precious metals belong at the base of the pyramid, not the top.
    - D. The client should first move into the aggressive investment tier.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: The pyramid builds from the base up. Speculating at the top without the security layer (insurance and a will) makes the whole plan unstable.
    </details>

20. While using the financial planning pyramid, the representative notices the client needs help drafting a will. The representative should:
    - A. Refer the client to a lawyer for the will.
    - B. Draft a simple will for the client to save costs.
    - C. Tell the client a will isn't needed if beneficiaries are named.
    - D. Skip the will and focus on investment recommendations.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: Representatives should only give advice they are licensed to provide and should refer clients to specialists — here, a lawyer — for everything else.
    </details>

21. The best way for a client to determine how much discretionary income is available for saving is to:
    - A. Estimate it from memory of past spending.
    - B. Review their net worth statement.
    - C. Prepare a household budget.
    - D. Review last year's tax return.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: A household budget lists monthly or annual inflows and outflows, which shows what is left for saving. A net worth statement is a snapshot of assets and liabilities, not cash flow.
    </details>

22. As a general guideline mentioned in the textbook, an emergency cash reserve should typically be:
    - A. One to two weeks of net income.
    - B. One to two years of net income.
    - C. One month of gross income.
    - D. Three to six months of net income.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: The common guideline is three to six months of net income, held in liquid form to cover unexpected expenses or a loss of income.
    </details>

23. A client has very little liquid savings and a low, unstable income, but wants to begin investing. Which type of fund should the representative direct them toward?
    - A. An equity growth fund.
    - B. A money market fund.
    - C. A long-term bond fund.
    - D. A balanced fund.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: With little cushion and unstable income, this client needs liquidity and stability. A money market fund offers both, while the others fluctuate in value.
    </details>

24. Which TWO of the following are among the general objectives typically listed on a mutual fund order form? (Choose TWO)
    - A. Safety of capital.
    - B. Tax-free income.
    - C. Guaranteed principal.
    - D. Capital growth.
    - E. Currency protection.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A, D
      Explanation: The three general objectives are safety of capital, current income, and capital growth.
    </details>

25. A representative completes a client's financial plan and considers the process finished. Is this correct?
    - A. Yes; a completed plan shouldn't be changed.
    - B. Yes, unless the client's income changes significantly.
    - C. No; the plan should be reviewed periodically as circumstances change.
    - D. No; the plan must be redone from scratch every year.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Financial planning is an ongoing process. Periodic review is the final step, so the plan can be revised as the client's circumstances and the economy change.
    </details>
