# Chapter 4: Getting to Know the Client

Original practice questions grounded in the CSI Investment Funds in Canada (IFC) textbook, Chapter 4: the financial planning process, KYC information requirements, investment objectives, net worth, risk profile (tolerance vs. capacity), the life-cycle hypothesis, and the financial planning pyramid.

---

1. Which of the following correctly lists steps in the financial planning process, in order?
    - A. Implementing recommendations, then establishing the client-advisor relationship.
    - B. Establishing the client-advisor relationship, collecting data, analyzing data, recommending strategies, implementing recommendations, and conducting a periodic review.
    - C. Conducting a periodic review before any data has been collected.
    - D. Analyzing data before establishing the client-advisor relationship.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
    </details>

2. According to the textbook, a financial plan that is to be created must, among other things:
    - A. Guarantee a minimum rate of return.
    - B. Be as intimidating as possible to motivate the client.
    - C. Ignore lifestyle changes once set.
    - D. Be achievable and accommodate changes in lifestyle and income level.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
    </details>

3. At a minimum, when opening an account for a new client, the KYC rule requires the representative to gather all of the following EXCEPT:
    - A. The client's investment needs and objectives.
    - B. The client's risk profile and investment time horizon.
    - C. The exact make and model of the client's car.
    - D. The client's financial circumstances, including annual income and net worth.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
    </details>

4. A client's order form indicates "safety of capital" as their primary objective. Which fund type is the ONLY one that meets this objective, according to the textbook?
    - A. A money market fund.
    - B. An equity growth fund.
    - C. A high-yield corporate bond fund.
    - D. A specialty sector fund.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
    </details>

5. A client seeking "current income" from mortgage and bond funds should generally be prepared to accept:
    - A. No investment risk whatsoever.
    - B. A shorter investment horizon than a money market fund investor.
    - C. Guaranteed capital preservation identical to a money market fund.
    - D. A longer investment horizon than a money market fund investor, since these funds are more volatile.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
    </details>

6. A client wants their portfolio to grow significantly in value over time and is willing to accept volatility to achieve this. This objective is best described as:
    - A. Safety of capital.
    - B. Capital growth.
    - C. Current income.
    - D. Tax minimization exclusively.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
    </details>

7. A 40-year-old client with two teenagers approaching university has very different short-term goals than a single 25-year-old with no dependents. This illustrates the impact of:
    - A. Investment knowledge.
    - B. Risk capacity alone.
    - C. Personal circumstances, such as marital status, dependents, and age.
    - D. The financial planning pyramid's top tier.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
    </details>

8. The amount of money left over after paying for all household bills and living expenses, available for savings, is called:
    - A. Discretionary income.
    - B. Net worth.
    - C. Total liabilities.
    - D. Gross income.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
    </details>

9. A client owns a home valued at $400,000, has $50,000 in investments, and owes $250,000 on the mortgage plus $10,000 in other debt. What is the client's approximate net worth?
    - A. $650,000.
    - B. $450,000.
    - C. $260,000.
    - D. $190,000.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
    </details>

10. A representative notes that a client's net worth rose sharply this year mainly because their home's market value increased. Why should the representative be cautious about relying on this figure alone?
    - A. Home values cannot legally be included in a net worth statement.
    - B. Net worth statements only apply to renters.
    - C. Real estate is an illiquid asset not readily convertible to cash, so a rising home value doesn't necessarily reflect increased accessible savings.
    - D. Rising home values always indicate the client is a poor saver.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
    </details>

11. A client is emotionally comfortable with significant volatility (high risk tolerance) but has very little savings and an unstable income (low risk capacity). Under NI 31-103, the client's overall risk profile should reflect:
    - A. The higher of the two measures.
    - B. The lower of the two measures.
    - C. An average of the two measures.
    - D. Risk tolerance only, since capacity is not considered.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
    </details>

12. Why does the textbook suggest that more knowledgeable investors are generally better able to bear investment risk?
    - A. Because they better understand the risk/return trade-off and how it should be reflected in their portfolio.
    - B. Because they are legally exempt from KYC requirements.
    - C. Because knowledgeable investors are guaranteed higher returns.
    - D. Because knowledge eliminates market risk entirely.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
    </details>

13. Which TWO of the following distinguish a typical Stage 2 (family commitment years) client from a Stage 1 (early earning years) client? (Choose TWO)
    - A. Stage 2 clients generally have more liquidity than Stage 1 clients.
    - B. Stage 2 clients are typically older than Stage 5 clients.
    - C. Stage 2 clients generally carry heavier financial commitments, such as mortgage and childcare costs.
    - D. Stage 1 clients typically require life insurance more urgently than Stage 2 clients.
    - E. Life insurance tends to become a requirement rather than a discretionary expense for Stage 2 clients.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C, E
    </details>

14. Clients in Stage 4 (nearing retirement) typically differ from Stage 3 clients in that Stage 4 clients:
    - A. Have heavier family commitments than Stage 3 clients.
    - B. Are generally more risk tolerant than Stage 3 clients.
    - C. Are usually younger than Stage 2 clients.
    - D. Have fewer family commitments and have become more risk averse, knowing they will soon rely on savings.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
    </details>

15. As a client moves into Stage 5 (retired), the textbook indicates their asset allocation will most likely shift toward:
    - A. A larger equity component to maximize growth.
    - B. Less risk overall, with a declining equity component in favour of fixed income and safety investments.
    - C. 100% speculative investments.
    - D. No change at all from Stage 4.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>

    </details>

16. A 62-year-old client turns out to have an unusually high tolerance for investment risk, inconsistent with the typical Stage 5 profile. The representative should:
    - A. Recognize that the life-cycle hypothesis is a general guide, not a rule that applies to every client, and adjust the recommendation to the client's actual profile.
    - B. Force the client's portfolio to match the "typical" Stage 5 allocation regardless of their actual risk tolerance.
    - C. Refuse to serve the client, since they don't fit the model.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>

17. In the financial planning pyramid, which of the following belongs at the base ("Security") level, before any investment tier is addressed?
    - A. Precious metals and IPOs.

    - C. Insurance and a will.
    - D. Commodities and derivatives.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
    </details>

18. Art, IPOs, over-the-counter securities, real estate, and precious metals sit at which level of the financial planning pyramid?
    - A. Security.

    </details>

19. A client wants to invest in precious metals (top of the pyramid) but has no will and no life insurance in place. What does the financial planning pyramid suggest about this situation?
    - A. This is perfectly fine, since the pyramid tiers are unrelated to each other.
    - B. The client's plan lacks a stable base, and the foundational security and independence levels should be addressed first.

    </details>

20. While using the financial planning pyramid with a client, the representative notices the client needs legal advice on drafting a will. The representative should:
    - A. Restrict their own advice to services they are licensed to provide and refer the client to a lawyer for the will.
    - B. Draft the will themselves to save the client money.
    - C. Ignore the issue, since wills are not part of financial planning.
    - D. Tell the client a will is unnecessary.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
    </details>

21. The best way for a client to determine how much discretionary income is available for savings is to:
