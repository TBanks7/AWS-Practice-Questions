# Chapter 11: Conservative Mutual Fund Products

Original practice questions grounded in the CSI Investment Funds in Canada (IFC) textbook, Chapter 11: money market funds and their yield calculations, mortgage funds, bond funds and duration, short-term bond funds, and preferred dividend funds.

---

1. According to the Canadian Investment Funds Standards Committee (CIFSC), a fund can be classified as a money market fund only if it invests at least what percentage of its net assets in money market securities?
    - A. 50%.
    - B. 75%.
    - C. 90%.
    - D. 95%.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: The CIFSC requires at least 95% of total net assets in money market securities for a fund to be classified as a money market fund.
    </details>

2. Money market securities are generally short-term, highly liquid fixed-income investments with maturities of:
    - A. More than 10 years.
    - B. 364 days or less.
    - C. Up to five years.
    - D. 90 days or less.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: Money market securities mature in one year (364 days) or less. The 90-day figure is the maximum average term to maturity for the whole portfolio, not for each security.
    </details>

3. A client assumes a money market fund carries the same deposit insurance as a bank term deposit. The representative should clarify that:
    - A. Money market funds are not CDIC-insured.
    - B. Money market funds have higher CDIC limits.
    - C. Both are CDIC-insured up to $100,000.
    - D. Only the money market fund is CDIC-insured.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: Money market funds are not insured by CDIC or anyone else. Their low risk comes from their holdings, not from insurance.
    </details>

4. National Instrument 81-102 requires that a money market fund's average term to maturity not exceed:
    - A. 364 days.
    - B. 30 days.
    - C. 90 days.
    - D. 180 days.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: The portfolio's average term to maturity can't exceed 90 days, even though individual securities may mature in up to 364 days.
    </details>

5. A money market fund's seven-day yield is calculated as:
    - A. Coupon rate ÷ market price.
    - B. Ending NAV × 365/7.
    - C. Initial NAV ÷ ending NAV.
    - D. (Ending NAV ÷ initial NAV) − 1.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: The seven-day yield is the percentage change in NAV over seven days. The current yield annualizes it (× 365/7), and the effective yield compounds it.
    </details>

6. Compared with a money market fund's current yield, its effective yield will generally be:
    - A. Lower, since it ignores compounding.
    - B. Higher, since it assumes weekly compounding.
    - C. Identical in all cases.
    - D. Lower, since it deducts the MER.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: Effective yield = (1 + seven-day yield)^(365/7) − 1, which assumes the weekly return compounds. Current yield just multiplies by 365/7, so effective yield is always higher.
    </details>

7. A mortgage's amortization period is the time needed to repay the entire principal. The mortgage's term refers to:
    - A. How long the current interest rate stays in effect.
    - B. The time needed to repay the entire principal.
    - C. The date the mortgage was first registered.
    - D. The period covered by mortgage insurance.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: The term is the period the interest rate and conditions apply, anywhere from six months to 10 years, after which the mortgage is renegotiated. The amortization period is usually much longer.
    </details>

8. A borrower who wants to be able to repay the mortgage in full at any time without an interest penalty should choose a(n):
    - A. Non-conventional mortgage.
    - B. Closed mortgage.
    - C. Open mortgage.
    - D. Variable-rate mortgage.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: An open mortgage can be paid off at any time without penalty. A closed mortgage charges a penalty for early repayment, usually in exchange for a lower rate.
    </details>

9. A residential mortgage exceeding 80% of the property's appraised value is a non-conventional mortgage and generally requires:
    - A. No insurance of any kind.
    - B. A shorter amortization period.
    - C. A lower interest rate.
    - D. Mortgage insurance.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: Mortgages above 80% loan-to-value must be insured (for example, NHA insurance), which can add up to 300 basis points to the borrower's cost.
    </details>

10. A mortgage fund holds a $100,000 mortgage bought at par when rates were 5%. If rates on new mortgages rise to 6%, the market value of the existing mortgage, and the fund's NAVPS, will:
    - A. Rise.
    - B. Fall.
    - C. Stay unchanged.
    - D. Rise, then fall.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: The 5% mortgage is less attractive when new mortgages pay 6%, so its market value falls, and the fund's NAVPS falls with it. Mortgage funds move inversely to rates, just like bonds.
    </details>

11. Which TWO of the following help explain why mortgage funds generally have low default risk? (Choose TWO)
    - A. Funds hold thousands of individual mortgages.
    - B. The Bank of Canada guarantees fund mortgages.
    - C. Most mortgages held are NHA-insured or privately insured.
    - D. Funds invest only in commercial mortgages.
    - E. Provinces repay any defaulted mortgage.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A, C
      Explanation: Holding thousands of mortgages spreads out the risk of any one default, and most mortgages are insured against default. Neither the Bank of Canada nor the provinces guarantee them.
    </details>

12. Mortgage funds are generally less sensitive to interest rates than bond funds partly because:
    - A. Mortgages aren't considered fixed-income securities.
    - B. Mortgage rates are set daily by the bond market.
    - C. Mortgages typically have longer terms than bonds.
    - D. Mortgage rates reset less often than bond yields.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: Lenders change mortgage rates less often than bond yields change in the market, and mortgage payments come monthly rather than semi-annually. Both reduce interest rate sensitivity.
    </details>

13. According to the textbook, which sequence ranks fixed-income fund types from LOWEST to HIGHEST volatility?
    - A. Bond, mortgage, money market, preferred dividend.
    - B. Money market, mortgage, bond, preferred dividend.
    - C. Mortgage, money market, bond, preferred dividend.
    - D. Money market, bond, mortgage, preferred dividend.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: From least to most volatile: money market, mortgage, bond, then preferred dividend funds.
    </details>

14. The single number that measures a bond portfolio's sensitivity to interest rate changes, expressed in years, is:
    - A. Duration.
    - B. Current yield.
    - C. Effective yield.
    - D. Term to maturity.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: Duration (sometimes called time-weighted maturity) reflects both term and coupon. The higher the duration, the more sensitive the price is to rate changes.
    </details>

15. A bond fund manager who expects interest rates to fall would most likely:
    - A. Shorten duration to reduce price risk.
    - B. Move the portfolio into T-bills.
    - C. Lengthen duration with longer, lower-coupon bonds.
    - D. Replace bonds with high-coupon short-term issues.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Longer, lower-coupon bonds gain the most when rates fall, so the manager lengthens duration. When rates are expected to rise, the manager shortens it.
    </details>

16. For all bonds except zero-coupon bonds, duration is:
    - A. Always greater than term to maturity.
    - B. Always equal to term to maturity.
    - C. Unrelated to term to maturity.
    - D. Always less than term to maturity.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: Coupon payments return some cash before maturity, which pulls duration below the term. A zero-coupon bond pays everything at maturity, so its duration equals its term.
    </details>

17. A client assumes bond funds are always stable, based on how bonds were viewed before the late 1970s. The representative should explain that:
    - A. Bond funds remain as stable today as they were then.
    - B. Greater rate volatility has made bond funds riskier.
    - C. Regulations now prevent bond funds from being volatile.
    - D. Only corporate bond funds have ever been volatile.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: Interest rates have been far more volatile since the late 1970s, so bond funds can at times be as risky as equities.
    </details>

18. Corporate bonds, especially those with lower credit ratings, generally offer higher yields than government bonds to:
    - A. Compensate investors for higher default risk.
    - B. Reflect a tax advantage over government bonds.
    - C. Match the returns on money market funds.
    - D. Offset currency risk on Canadian holdings.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: Investors demand extra yield for taking on more credit (default) risk. The lower the rating, the bigger the premium.
    </details>

19. A short-term bond fund is best described as:
    - A. A money market fund under another name.
    - B. An equity fund with short holding periods.
    - C. A hybrid of a money market fund and a bond fund.
    - D. A fund that holds only corporate bonds.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Short-term bond funds try to preserve capital while earning more income than money market funds. Because their holdings don't meet the maturity rules, they can't be called money market funds.
    </details>

20. Which TWO of the following explain why regulators restrict which funds may be called "money market funds"? (Choose TWO)
    - A. To let any short-term bond fund use the name.
    - B. To prevent investors assuming a fund is low risk because of its name.
    - C. Because money market funds are being phased out.
    - D. Because only funds meeting maturity criteria should carry that low-risk label.
    - E. Because money market funds are CDIC-insured.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B, D
      Explanation: The name implies very low risk, so regulators reserve it for funds that meet the maturity and composition rules. Otherwise investors could be misled.
    </details>

21. The primary objective of a preferred dividend fund is:
    - A. Dividend income and capital preservation.
    - B. Maximum short-term capital gains.
    - C. Tracking a broad equity index.
    - D. Interest income from government bonds.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: Preferred dividend funds aim for steady dividend income while preserving capital, with limited potential for capital gains.
    </details>

22. Whether a fund is called a "preferred dividend fund" depends primarily on:
    - A. The number of unitholders in the fund.
    - B. A minimum percentage held in preferred shares.
    - C. The stability of its expected dividend income.
    - D. Whether it invests outside Canada.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: The label depends on how stable the fund's dividend income is expected to be, not on a strict portfolio percentage. A fund that is mostly in common shares is usually just called a dividend fund.
    </details>

23. One of the two main reasons for preferring dividend income over the interest income from bond funds is that:
    - A. Dividend income is tax-exempt in Canada.
    - B. Interest income receives the dividend tax credit.
    - C. Bond interest is always higher than dividends.
    - D. Canadian dividends receive the dividend tax credit.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: Thanks to the dividend tax credit, eligible Canadian dividends are taxed less heavily than interest. The other reason is that riskier dividend-paying securities are expected to earn higher returns.
    </details>

24. Preferred shares are generally expected to offer higher income yields than government bonds because they:
    - A. Are guaranteed by the federal government.
    - B. Are riskier than government bonds.
    - C. Are less risky than government bonds.
    - D. Are protected from market fluctuations.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: Preferred shareholders rank behind creditors, and their dividends aren't guaranteed. That extra risk calls for a higher expected return.
    </details>

25. A preferred dividend fund that holds only preferred shares rated P1 or P2 is restricting itself to:
    - A. The two highest preferred share ratings.
    - B. The two lowest preferred share ratings.
    - C. Ratings used for government bonds.
    - D. Measures of a fund's MER.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: P1 and P2 are the top two quality ratings for preferred shares, so the fund is limiting itself to the highest-quality issues.
    </details>

26. A fixed-income-oriented fund holds mostly common shares but still earns a stable stream of dividend income. This type of fund is typically called a:
    - A. Money market fund.
    - B. Mortgage fund.
    - C. Dividend fund.
    - D. Short-term bond fund.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: When a fund's dividend income comes mainly from common shares, it's usually called a dividend fund rather than a preferred dividend fund.
    </details>

27. In the case study, Terry recommends a preferred dividend fund to John, a retiree with already-high investment income, primarily because it offers:
    - A. Guaranteed capital growth with no income.
    - B. Complete protection from market risk.
    - C. Exposure to small-cap growth stocks.
    - D. Stable income taxed at a lower rate.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: John needs income and already pays a lot of tax. Dividend income that qualifies for the dividend tax credit is taxed less than interest from bond or mortgage funds.
    </details>

28. Over the textbook's hypothetical 15-year period, which ranking of average annual returns, from lowest to highest, is correct?
    - A. Bond, mortgage, money market.
    - B. Money market, mortgage, bond.
    - C. Mortgage, bond, money market.
    - D. Money market, bond, mortgage.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: Returns rose with risk: money market funds (about 3.2%), then mortgage funds (about 4.4%), then bond funds (about 5.3%).
    </details>
