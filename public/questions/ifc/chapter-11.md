# Chapter 11: Conservative Mutual Fund Products

Original practice questions grounded in the CSI Investment Funds in Canada (IFC) textbook, Chapter 11: money market funds and their yield calculations, mortgage funds, bond funds and duration, short-term bond funds, and preferred dividend funds.

---

1. According to the Canadian Investment Funds Standards Committee (CIFSC), a fund can be classified as a money market fund only if it invests at least what percentage of its total net assets in money market securities?
    - A. 50%.
    - B. 75%.
    - C. 25%.
    - D. 95%.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
    </details>

2. Money market securities are generally defined as short-term, highly liquid, fixed-income investments with maturities of:
    - A. More than 10 years.
    - B. 364 days or less.
    - C. Exactly 5 years.
    - D. There is no maximum maturity.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
    </details>

3. A client compares a money market mutual fund to a bank term deposit and assumes both carry identical deposit insurance protection. The representative should clarify that:
    - A. Money market funds are not insured by CDIC or any other insurer, unlike an insured bank deposit.
    - B. Money market funds carry more CDIC coverage than term deposits.
    - C. Both products are always insured identically.
    - D. Term deposits are never insured, unlike money market funds.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
    </details>

4. National Instrument 81-102 requires that a money market fund's average term to maturity not exceed:
    - A. 364 days.
    - B. 30 days.
    - C. 90 days.
    - D. 10 years.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
    </details>

5. A money market fund's seven-day yield is calculated as:
    - A. Coupon rate divided by market price.
    - B. Ending NAV multiplied by 365/7.
    - C. Beginning NAV divided by ending NAV.
    - D. (Ending Net Asset Value / Initial Net Asset Value) minus 1.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
    </details>

6. Comparing a money market fund's current yield to its effective yield, the effective yield will generally be:
    - A. Lower than the current yield, since it ignores compounding.
    - B. Higher than the current yield, since it assumes the seven-day return compounds weekly over a year.
    - C. Identical to the current yield in all cases.
    - D. Impossible to calculate from the seven-day yield.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
    </details>

7. A mortgage's amortization period refers to the time it takes to pay off the entire principal, while the mortgage's term refers to:
    - A. The period during which a particular interest rate stays in effect, after which the mortgage is renegotiated.
    - B. The exact same thing as the amortization period.
    - C. The date the mortgage was first issued.
    - D. The insurance premium charged on the mortgage.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
    </details>

8. A borrower who wants the flexibility to repay their mortgage in full at any time, without paying an interest penalty, should choose a(n):
    - A. Non-conventional mortgage.
    - B. Closed mortgage.
    - C. Open mortgage.
    - D. NHA-insured mortgage exclusively.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
    </details>

9. A residential mortgage exceeding 80% of the appraised value of the property is classified as a non-conventional mortgage and generally requires:
    - A. No insurance of any kind.
    - B. A lower interest rate than a conventional mortgage.
    - C. Automatic default within one year.
    - D. Mortgage insurance, which can raise the mortgage's cost by up to 300 basis points.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
    </details>

10. A mortgage fund manager holds a $100,000 mortgage purchased at par when rates were 5%. If mortgage rates for newly negotiated mortgages suddenly rise to 6%, the market value of that existing 5% mortgage, and the fund's NAVPS, will:
    - A. Rise, since higher rates always benefit existing fixed-income holdings.
    - B. Fall, since the mortgage's fixed 5% payments become less attractive relative to new 6% mortgages.
    - C. Remain completely unchanged.
    - D. Be unaffected until the mortgage's term ends.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
    </details>

11. Which TWO of the following help explain why mortgage funds generally have low default risk? (Choose TWO)
    - A. A typical mortgage fund is highly diversified, sometimes holding 10,000 to 15,000 individual mortgages.
    - B. Mortgage funds are guaranteed against loss by the Bank of Canada.
    - C. Most mortgages held are NHA-insured or privately insured against default.
    - D. Mortgage funds never invest in residential mortgages.
    - E. Provincial governments directly repay any defaulted mortgage in a fund's portfolio.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A, C
    </details>

12. Mortgage funds are generally expected to have lower interest rate sensitivity than bond funds partly because:
    - A. Mortgages are not considered fixed-income securities.
    - B. Bond interest is paid monthly, while mortgage interest is paid semi-annually.
    - C. Mortgage rates change daily, just like bond yields.
    - D. Mortgage rates change less frequently than bond yields, and mortgage interest is typically paid monthly rather than semi-annually.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>

    </details>

13. According to the textbook, the volatility of returns among the four fixed-income mutual fund types, from LOWEST to HIGHEST, is generally:
    - A. Bond funds, mortgage funds, money market funds, preferred dividend funds.

14. The single number used to measure a bond or bond portfolio's sensitivity to changes in interest rates, expressed in years and sometimes called time-weighted maturity, is known as:

    - D. Amortized cost.

15. A bond fund manager who believes interest rates are about to fall would most likely:
    - A. Shorten the portfolio's duration to protect against losses.
    - B. Sell all bonds and hold only cash.

    </details>

16. For all bonds except zero-coupon bonds, a bond's duration is:
    - A. Always greater than its term to maturity.

    </details>

17. A client assumes bond funds are always low-risk, stable investments, based on how bonds were perceived before the late 1970s. The representative should explain that:

18. Corporate bonds, especially those with lower credit ratings, generally offer better yields than government bonds primarily to:

19. A short-term bond fund is best described as:
