# Chapter 6: Tax and Retirement Planning

Original practice questions grounded in the CSI Investment Funds in Canada (IFC) textbook, Chapter 6: taxation of investment income, government and employer pension plans, RRSPs, RRIFs, TFSAs, and RESPs/CESGs.

---

1. A client holds a GIC that pays annual interest. For tax purposes, this interest income is:
    - A. Only 50% taxable, like a capital gain.
    - B. Taxed in full at the client's marginal tax rate, with no preferential treatment.
    - C. Entirely tax-exempt.
    - D. Eligible for the dividend tax credit.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
    </details>

2. An investor realizes a $10,000 capital gain on the sale of shares held outside a registered account. What amount is included in their taxable income?
    - A. $10,000.
    - B. $0.
    - C. $2,000.
    - D. $5,000, since only 50% of a capital gain is taxable.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
    </details>

3. Eligible Canadian dividends are "grossed-up" before being included in a taxpayer's income. This mechanism exists primarily to:
    - A. Approximate the pre-tax corporate earnings the dividend came from, so a matching dividend tax credit can offset the tax the corporation already paid.
    - B. Increase the taxpayer's total tax bill with no offsetting credit.
    - C. Apply exclusively to foreign dividends.
    - D. Replace the need to report the dividend at all.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
    </details>

4. Compared to eligible dividends from Canadian corporations, dividends received from foreign corporations are:
    - A. Also eligible for the Canadian dividend tax credit.
    - B. Entirely tax-exempt.
    - C. Generally taxed as regular income, with no dividend tax credit, similar to interest income.
    - D. Only 50% taxable, like a capital gain.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
    </details>

5. An investor incurs a capital loss on a stock sale in a non-registered account. Can this capital loss be used to reduce their employment income for the year?
    - A. Yes, capital losses can offset any type of income.
    - B. No, capital losses can generally only be applied against capital gains, not against employment, interest, or dividend income.
    - C. Yes, but only up to $2,000.
    - D. No, capital losses can never be used for any tax purpose.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
    </details>

6. Which statement about contributions to the Canada Pension Plan (or Quebec Pension Plan for Quebec residents) is correct?
    - A. Contributions are voluntary and can be withdrawn at any time before retirement.
    - B. Only self-employed individuals are required to contribute.
    - C. The pension entitlement is forfeited if the contributor permanently leaves Canada.
    - D. Contributions are automatic for those earning employment or self-employment income, and the resulting pension entitlement is irrevocable.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
    </details>

7. A client wants to begin receiving CPP payments at age 60 instead of waiting until 65. What effect will this have on their monthly pension amount?
    - A. It will be reduced, since starting CPP before 65 permanently lowers the monthly amount.
    - B. It will be unaffected, since CPP pays the same amount regardless of start age.
    - C. It will automatically increase to compensate for the earlier start.
    - D. It is not permitted under any circumstances.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
    </details>

8. To qualify for a full Old Age Security pension, an applicant generally must have been a resident of Canada for at least how many years after turning 18?
    - A. 10 years.
    - B. 18 years.
    - C. 40 years.
    - D. 65 years.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
    </details>

9. A high-income retiree's Old Age Security payments are reduced because their net income exceeds a set threshold. This reduction is known as:
    - A. A dividend tax credit.
    - B. A pension adjustment.
    - C. A deemed disposition.
    - D. The OAS clawback, a repayment provision under the Income Tax Act.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
    </details>

10. In which type of registered pension plan does the plan member know, in advance, the formula that will determine their pension amount at retirement?
    - A. A defined contribution (money purchase) plan.
    - B. A defined benefit plan.
    - C. A Pooled Registered Pension Plan.
    - D. A Tax-Free Savings Account.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
    </details>

11. An employee is covered by a flat benefit pension plan paying $20 per month for each year of service. After 25 years of service, what monthly pension will they receive?
    - A. $250.
    - B. $20.
    - C. $500 ($20 x 25 years).
    - D. $2,500.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
    </details>

12. A key disadvantage of a flat benefit pension plan is that:
    - A. The flat dollar amount must be periodically renegotiated, or its real value will erode due to inflation.
    - B. It cannot be offered to unionized employees.
    - C. It automatically differentiates among employees' earnings levels.
    - D. It cannot be paid in addition to CPP/QPP or OAS.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
    </details>

13. An employee in a career average pension plan earns a benefit of 2% of average annualized salary per year of service. Over a 20-year career, their average annual salary was $50,000. What is their annual pension at retirement?
    - A. $50,000.
    - B. $2,000.
    - C. $10,000.
    - D. $20,000 ($50,000 x 2% x 20 years).

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
    </details>

14. A final average pension plan bases benefits on an employee's average earnings during their final years of service (such as the best 5 of the last 10 years) rather than their entire career average, primarily because:
    - A. It is easier to administer than a career average plan.
    - B. It better protects the pension against inflation, since it reflects earnings closer to retirement.
    - C. It always produces a lower pension than a career average plan.
    - D. It eliminates the need for employer contributions.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
    </details>

15. A key disadvantage of a defined contribution (money purchase) pension plan, compared to a defined benefit plan, is that:
    - A. The employer bears all investment risk.
    - B. Contributions cannot be invested in a range of vehicles.
    - C. The final pension amount is unknown until retirement, since it depends on investment performance over the life of the plan.
    - D. Employees cannot direct how their contributions are invested.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
    </details>

16. Combined employer and employee contributions to a defined contribution pension plan cannot exceed the lesser of the annual dollar limit and:
    - A. 18% of the employee's current year compensation.
    - B. 50% of the employee's current year compensation.
    - C. A fixed $10,000 regardless of income.
    - D. The employee's total lifetime earnings.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
    </details>

17. A client earned $80,000 last year and has $5,000 of unused RRSP contribution room carried forward, with no pension adjustment. Assuming the 18% figure is below the annual dollar limit, what is their maximum RRSP contribution this year?
    - A. $80,000.
    - B. $14,400.
    - C. $5,000.
    - D. $19,400 (18% of $80,000 = $14,400, plus $5,000 carry-forward).

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
    </details>

18. For an RRSP contribution to be deductible for a given taxation year, it must generally be made:
    - A. Only during the calendar year itself, with no grace period.
    - B. During the taxation year or within 60 days after the end of that year.
    - C. Within 6 months after the end of the taxation year.
    - D. At any time, with no deadline whatsoever.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
    </details>

19. Which TWO of the following correctly describe the RRSP over-contribution rules? (Choose TWO)
    - A. Over-contributions of up to $2,000 may be made without triggering a penalty tax.
    - B. Any over-contribution, even $1, triggers an immediate penalty.
    - C. A penalty tax of 1% per month applies to the portion of an over-contribution that exceeds $2,000.
    - D. Over-contributions are always fully tax-deductible regardless of amount.
    - E. RRSP over-contributions can never occur under current legislation.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A, C
    </details>

20. A client contributes mutual fund units they already own to their self-directed RRSP, rather than contributing cash. For tax purposes, this contribution is treated as:
    - A. A tax-free transfer with no tax consequences.
    - B. A capital loss regardless of the units' value.
    - C. Exempt from any capital gains reporting.
    - D. A deemed disposition at fair market value, potentially triggering a taxable capital gain (though any resulting capital loss is deemed nil).

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
    </details>

21. A husband contributes to his wife's spousal RRSP and claims the tax deduction. If the wife withdraws those funds within the same year or the following two calendar years, the withdrawal is generally taxed as income to:
    - A. The wife, since it is her RRSP.
    - B. The husband (the contributor), under the spousal RRSP attribution rule.
    - C. Neither spouse, since spousal withdrawals are always tax-free.
    - D. Their children, if any are named as beneficiaries.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
    </details>

22. By what age must an individual's RRSP be converted to a RRIF, used to purchase an annuity, or collapsed as a taxable lump sum?

    - B. By the end of the year they turn 65.

    - D. There is no mandatory conversion age.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>

23. A RRIF holder is required, each year, to withdraw:
    - A. A fixed dollar amount that never changes.
    - B. Nothing, since RRIF withdrawals are entirely optional.
