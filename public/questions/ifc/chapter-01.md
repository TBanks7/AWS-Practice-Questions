# Chapter 1: The Role of the Mutual Fund Sales Representative

Original practice questions grounded in the CSI Investment Funds in Canada (IFC) textbook, Chapter 1: mutual fund industry history, the value of licensing, client service, legal/ethical/professional responsibility, ethics vs. compliance, fiduciary duty, disclosure, the Client Focused Reforms, suitability, KYC, and KYP.

---

1. A colleague claims that mutual funds are a purely modern invention, no more than 50 years old. Is this accurate?
    - A. Yes; the first mutual funds were launched in Canada in the 1930s.
    - B. Yes; mutual funds originated in the United States after World War II.
    - C. No; their origins are traced to the Netherlands in 1774 or England in the mid-1800s.
    - D. No; their origins are traced to the opening of the Toronto Stock Exchange in 1861.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Depending on the source, historians trace the first mutual funds to the Netherlands in 1774 or England in the mid-1800s. Canada's first funds appeared much later, in the 1930s.
    </details>

2. According to the industry's historical timeline, roughly how many mutual funds were operating in Canada during the 1930s?
    - A. Three.
    - B. Three thousand.
    - C. Thirty.
    - D. Three hundred.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: Only three funds were operating in Canada in the 1930s. The industry grew slowly to about 30 funds by the 1960s before expanding rapidly in later decades.
    </details>

3. Which factor is most credited with the rapid growth of Canada's mutual fund industry during the 1980s?
    - A. The introduction of the Tax-Free Savings Account.
    - B. The launch of the first Canadian index funds.
    - C. The creation of a single national securities regulator.
    - D. Chartered banks entering the mutual fund business.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: When the chartered banks entered the business in the 1980s, their branch networks brought funds to a mass audience. TFSAs didn't arrive until 2009, and Canada still has no single national securities regulator.
    </details>

4. A prospective client asks why mutual funds became so widely used by everyday investors. The best explanation is that they:
    - A. Offered a guaranteed return that was higher than bank deposits.
    - B. Gave small investors affordable access to professional management and diversification.
    - C. Were exempt from the Know Your Client rules that apply to stocks and bonds.
    - D. Were insured against loss by the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: Pooling money lets investors with modest sums share a professionally managed, diversified portfolio at relatively low cost. Mutual funds have no guaranteed return, are subject to KYC, and are not CDIC-insured.
    </details>

5. Before an individual may be licensed to sell mutual funds in Canada, they must first satisfy:
    - A. Educational, employment, and work experience criteria.
    - B. A university degree in finance, economics, or commerce.
    - C. A minimum personal net worth set by the provincial regulator.
    - D. Two years of supervised experience at a bank branch.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: Licensing requires meeting education (for example, passing a recognized course like the IFC), employment (sponsorship by a dealer), and experience criteria. No degree or net worth test is required.
    </details>

6. A newly licensed representative assumes that no further study will ever be required. Is this correct?
    - A. Yes; once registered, no further education is required.
    - B. Yes, unless the representative moves to a different dealer.
    - C. No; continuing education may be required to maintain the licence.
    - D. No; the licensing exam must be rewritten every three years.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Representatives may have to complete continuing education courses to keep their licence. As long as registration is maintained, there is no requirement to periodically rewrite the licensing exam.
    </details>

7. The textbook describes client service as fully understanding client needs and then:
    - A. Recommending the products with the strongest recent performance.
    - B. Matching the client with the lowest-cost products available.
    - C. Processing the client's instructions quickly and accurately.
    - D. Identifying the "right" solutions to satisfy those needs.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: Client service means understanding the client's needs and then identifying the right solutions for them. Performance, cost, and speed all matter, but none of them defines client service.
    </details>

8. Which TWO of the following are given as reasons why client service is a central concern for mutual fund sales representatives? (Choose TWO)
    - A. Clients who receive good service are legally barred from switching dealers.
    - B. Mutual fund sales are subject to regulations and disclosure requirements that demand a specialized approach.
    - C. Mutual funds carry more risk than any other retail financial product.
    - D. Representatives' compensation is tied directly to client satisfaction surveys.
    - E. The rapidly changing financial services environment requires knowing the characteristics and purpose of many products.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B, E
      Explanation: The textbook cites two reasons: the sales regulations and disclosure requirements that apply to mutual funds, and a fast-changing industry that requires reps to understand many different products.
    </details>

9. A representative accepts a client's order for a fund without checking whether it fits the client's objectives, time horizon, or risk profile. Which responsibility is most directly breached?
    - A. Legal responsibility.
    - B. Ethical responsibility.
    - C. Professional responsibility.
    - D. None, since the client initiated the order.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: Provincial securities acts make it a legal responsibility to ensure that any investment recommended, or any order accepted, is suitable. This applies to client-initiated orders too.
    </details>

10. A representative believes a lower-fee fund would serve a client better, but recommends a higher-fee in-house fund because their manager encourages it. This choice most directly conflicts with which responsibility?
    - A. Professional responsibility.
    - B. Legal responsibility.
    - C. Compliance responsibility.
    - D. Ethical responsibility.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: Ethical responsibility means putting the client's needs ahead of your own and your dealer's. Favouring the in-house fund to please a manager breaches that, even if the fund is technically suitable.
    </details>

11. "Professional responsibility," as distinguished from legal and ethical responsibility, refers to a representative's duty to:
    - A. Meet all suitability requirements in securities law.
    - B. Place clients' needs ahead of personal interests.
    - C. Provide the best client service possible.
    - D. Follow the dealer's written policies and procedures.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: The three responsibilities are legal (ensure suitability), ethical (put the client first), and professional (provide the best client service possible). Options A and B describe the other two.
    </details>

12. A representative follows every rule in the dealer's compliance manual, yet a client feels misled by a technically accurate but incomplete explanation. This scenario illustrates the difference between:
    - A. Legal responsibility and professional responsibility.
    - B. Compliance and ethical conduct.
    - C. Know Your Client and Know Your Product.
    - D. Suitability and disclosure.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: Compliance means following the rules; ethical conduct means honouring both the letter and the spirit of the law. An explanation that is technically accurate but misleading meets the letter but not the spirit.
    </details>

13. Is it possible for a representative to behave unethically while still complying with every applicable rule?
    - A. Yes, because rules cannot anticipate every situation a representative will face.
    - B. No, because compliance with the rules is the definition of ethical conduct.
    - C. No, because regulators have codified all ethical standards into rules.
    - D. Yes, but only when the representative acts outside their registration category.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: Rules only cover the most common situations, and ethics goes beyond what the rules prescribe. So it is possible to comply with every rule and still act unethically.
    </details>

14. Which combination of factors is most likely to establish a fiduciary duty between a representative and a client?
    - A. The size of the account and the fees the client pays.
    - B. The length of the relationship and the number of trades.
    - C. Whether the account is registered or non-registered.
    - D. The client's reliance on the advice and the client's vulnerability.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: A fiduciary duty is more likely to exist when the client relies heavily on the representative's advice and is vulnerable. Account size, tenure, and account type are not the deciding factors.
    </details>

15. A retired couple with little investment experience asked for secure monthly income. Their advisor placed most of their money in equity funds, and after a severe downturn a court found the firm liable. What was the core failure?
    - A. The firm charged a management fee above the industry average.
    - B. The firm failed to follow the KYC rule and select suitable investments.
    - C. The firm failed to deliver the Fund Facts document before the purchase.
    - D. The firm failed to diversify across more than one fund company.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: The advisor didn't properly apply the KYC rule to inexperienced retirees who needed secure income, and put them in volatile equity funds that were unsuitable for them. That suitability failure was the basis of the finding.
    </details>

16. A client asks why the document summarizing their new fund is so short. The representative correctly explains that the Fund Facts document is designed to be:
    - A. A complete legal disclosure of every aspect of the fund.
    - B. An optional summary provided only if the client asks for it.
    - C. No more than four pages, highlighting key information.
    - D. A marketing piece prepared at the dealer's discretion.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Fund Facts is a short, plain-language document of no more than four pages (two double-sided pages) covering key information like risk, costs, and past performance. It must be delivered to the client; it isn't optional.
    </details>

17. If that same client wants more detail than the Fund Facts document provides, the representative should explain that the fund's prospectus:
    - A. Provides greater detail and is much longer than the Fund Facts.
    - B. Is a shorter summary derived from the Fund Facts.
    - C. Is available only to institutional investors.
    - D. Replaces the Fund Facts for all future purchases.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: The prospectus goes into much more detail than the Fund Facts and is considerably longer. Retail investors can get it on request.
    </details>

18. The Client Focused Reforms were introduced through final amendments the Canadian Securities Administrators released on October 3, 2019, to:
    - A. National Instrument 81-102.
    - B. National Instrument 81-101.
    - C. National Instrument 23-101.
    - D. National Instrument 31-103.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: The CFRs amended NI 31-103, which sets out registration requirements and ongoing registrant obligations. NI 81-101 and 81-102 deal with mutual fund disclosure and operations.
    </details>

19. A new representative lists the goals of the Client Focused Reforms to a colleague. Which statement is INCORRECT?
    - A. The reforms aim to better align registrants' interests with those of their clients.
    - B. The reforms aim to replace the Know Your Client rule with a product-based test.
    - C. The reforms aim to improve outcomes for clients.
    - D. The reforms aim to clarify the nature and terms of the client-registrant relationship.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: The CFRs strengthened KYC, adding an expanded list of required information and periodic reviews, rather than replacing it. The other three statements are stated goals of the reforms.
    </details>

20. The Client Focused Reforms are often summarized around three pillars of registrant expectations. These are:
    - A. Licensing, compliance, and disclosure.
    - B. Fund Facts, prospectus, and the annual information form.
    - C. Suitability, Know Your Client, and Know Your Product.
    - D. KYC, anti-money laundering rules, and privacy law.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Suitability, KYC, and KYP are the three pillars, and they depend on each other. You can't make a suitable recommendation without knowing both the client and the product.
    </details>

21. For a recommendation to satisfy the suitability requirement, it must be based on the representative's understanding of:
    - A. The client's personal and financial situation only.
    - B. The features of the products being recommended only.
    - C. The dealer's approved product list and sales targets.
    - D. Both the client's situation and the products being recommended.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: Suitability requires understanding both the client (their personal and financial situation and objectives) and the products being recommended. Knowing only one side isn't enough.
    </details>

22. The Know Your Client rule requires a representative to take reasonable steps to learn the essential facts relevant to:
    - A. Every client and every order.
    - B. Every client, at account opening only.
    - C. Every order above a set dollar threshold.
    - D. Clients who are first-time investors.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: KYC applies to every client and every order, not just at account opening and not only above a minimum dollar amount.
    </details>

23. A client says, "Just place the order — I'm not answering any more questions, or I'm taking my business elsewhere." The representative still cannot assess suitability. What should the representative do?
    - A. Accept the order but mark it as unsolicited.
    - B. Accept the order once the client signs a waiver.
    - C. Decline the order until KYC information is provided.
    - D. Accept the order but limit it to a money market fund.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Without KYC information the representative can't judge suitability, and accepting the order would breach securities law. Marking it unsolicited or getting a waiver doesn't remove that obligation.
    </details>

24. "Knowing your client" covers each of the following EXCEPT the client's:
    - A. Personal and financial circumstances.
    - B. Preferred fund company or brand.
    - C. Investment needs, objectives, and knowledge.
    - D. Risk profile and investment time horizon.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: The six KYC elements are personal circumstances, financial circumstances, investment needs and objectives, investment knowledge, risk profile, and time horizon. A preference for a fund brand is not one of them.
    </details>

25. Beyond understanding a fund's structure, features, risks, and costs, the Know Your Product rule also requires that a representative:
    - A. Personally hold units of each fund they recommend.
    - B. Compare each fund against every fund on the market.
    - C. Read each fund's full prospectus before every trade.
    - D. Recommend only investments approved by their Member firm.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: KYP requires understanding the product and recommending only investments the Member (the dealer) has approved. It does not require personal ownership or a market-wide comparison.
    </details>

26. A client asks their mutual fund representative to build a full household budget and long-term financial plan. The representative should recognize that:
    - A. This is beyond their role, which does not include full financial planning.
    - B. This is part of the KYC process and must be completed.
    - C. This may be provided once the client signs a consent form.
    - D. This is required before any fund can be recommended.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: A mutual fund representative guides fund selection based on suitability. Building full financial plans and budgets is a financial planner's role, so the representative should refer the client.
    </details>

27. A client asks their mutual-fund-only representative to buy shares of an individual stock. The representative should:
    - A. Place the trade because the client requested it directly.
    - B. Have a colleague place the trade under their supervision.
    - C. Explain the restriction and refer the client appropriately.
    - D. Recommend an equity fund that holds that stock instead.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Registration limited to mutual funds means the representative can only deal in mutual funds. A request for individual stocks has to be referred to someone registered to trade them.
    </details>

28. A 29-year-old client is saving for a house down payment he expects to need in two to three years. Given this time horizon alone, which fund type should generally be ruled out?
    - A. A short-term bond fund.
    - B. An equity mutual fund.
    - C. A money market fund.
    - D. A mortgage fund.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: Equity funds can fall sharply over a two-to-three-year period, which could force the client to sell at a loss just when he needs the down payment. Lower-volatility funds fit a short, fixed horizon better.
    </details>

29. Given that same client's moderate risk profile, limited investment knowledge, and short time horizon, which allocation best reflects a suitable recommendation?
    - A. 100% in an equity fund, as the client initially requested.
    - B. Mostly equity funds, with a small bond fund allocation.
    - C. An even split between equity and bond funds.
    - D. Mostly money market, with smaller bond and equity allocations.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: In the textbook example, the short horizon, moderate risk profile, and limited knowledge led to roughly 85% money market, 10% bond, and 5% equity, even though the client first asked for an equity fund.
    </details>

30. While reviewing a long-standing client's account, a representative notices the portfolio has drifted well away from its target asset allocation since the last review. The appropriate response is to:
    - A. Recommend rebalancing back toward the strategic asset allocation.
    - B. Leave the portfolio unchanged, since the client hasn't complained.
    - C. Move the portfolio into the best-performing fund it holds.
    - D. Wait until the next scheduled annual review to discuss it.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: Drift can leave the client carrying a level of risk that no longer matches their profile. As part of ongoing suitability, the representative should recommend rebalancing to the strategic allocation, as in the Mary case study.
    </details>
