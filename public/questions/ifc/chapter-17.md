# Chapter 17: Mutual Fund Dealer Regulation

Original practice questions grounded in the CSI Investment Funds in Canada (IFC) textbook, Chapter 17: securities administrators, the MFDA and AMF, registration requirements, the Client Focused Reforms (KYC/KYP/suitability), account types and referral arrangements, prohibited selling practices, client communications and complaints, privacy law (PIPEDA), and anti-money laundering/terrorist financing legislation.

---

1. The securities administrators of each Canadian province and territory collectively make up the:
    - A. MFDA.
    - B. FINTRAC.
    - C. Canadian Securities Administrators (CSA).
    - D. FATF.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
    </details>

2. According to the textbook, the operations of provincial and territorial securities administrators break down into three main categories:

11. Which TWO of the following KYC-related requirements were introduced or clarified under the Client Focused Reforms? (Choose TWO)
    - A. KYC information need only ever be collected once, at account opening, with no further review.

12. A client has a high risk tolerance (willingness) but a low risk capacity (ability to endure loss). Under NI 31-103 guidance, the client's overall risk profile should reflect:

17. A dealing representative tells a client the exact NAVPS their order will receive before the fund's daily valuation is complete, and later backdates an order to obtain a more favourable prior-day price. This is:

19. A representative promises a client that a fund's NAVPS will reach a specific dollar value within a year. This is:
