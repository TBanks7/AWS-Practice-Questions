# Chapter 17: Mutual Fund Dealer Regulation

Original practice questions grounded in the CSI Investment Funds in Canada (IFC) textbook, Chapter 17: securities administrators, the MFDA and AMF, registration requirements, the Client Focused Reforms (KYC/KYP/suitability), account types and referral arrangements, prohibited selling practices, client communications and complaints, privacy law (PIPEDA), and anti-money laundering/terrorist financing legislation.

---

1. The securities administrators of each Canadian province and territory collectively make up:
    - A. The Mutual Fund Dealers Association.
    - B. The Investment Industry Regulatory Organization.
    - C. The Canadian Securities Administrators.
    - D. The Financial Action Task Force.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: The CSA is the umbrella group of the provincial and territorial securities regulators. It coordinates and harmonizes regulation across Canada. The MFDA and IIROC are SROs, not regulators' umbrella groups.
    </details>

2. According to the textbook, the operations of provincial and territorial securities administrators fall into three main categories:
    - A. Registration, disclosure, and enforcement.
    - B. Taxation, licensing, and advertising.
    - C. Underwriting, custody, and settlement.
    - D. KYC, KYP, and suitability.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: Securities administrators register firms and individuals, require disclosure, and enforce the law through investigation and prosecution.
    </details>

3. The main difference between a National Instrument and a National Policy is that:
    - A. A National Policy has the force of law; an Instrument doesn't.
    - B. They are identical in legal effect.
    - C. National Instruments apply only in Quebec.
    - D. A National Instrument has the force of law; a Policy is guidance.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: National Instruments are rules with the force of law in the participating provinces. National Policies explain how regulators interpret and apply those rules.
    </details>

4. The Mutual Fund Dealers Association (MFDA) is the self-regulatory organization for mutual fund dealers:
    - A. In Quebec only.
    - B. Across Canada except Quebec.
    - C. In every province, including Quebec.
    - D. Only for dealers that are also IIROC members.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: In Quebec the AMF is the primary regulator. The MFDA doesn't regulate dealers that are already IIROC members.
    </details>

5. The MFDA Investor Protection Corporation (MFDA IPC), with coverage limited to $1,000,000 per customer account, protects against losses arising from:
    - A. Market declines.
    - B. Unsuitable recommendations.
    - C. The insolvency of an MFDA member dealer.
    - D. The default of a fund manager.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: MFDA IPC covers client property lost because a member dealer becomes insolvent. It doesn't cover market losses, unsuitable investments, or the failure of a fund or its manager.
    </details>

6. The person responsible for making sure dealing representatives at a branch deal fairly, honestly, and in good faith, and comply with securities law and dealer policy, is the:
    - A. Branch compliance officer.
    - B. Fund manager.
    - C. Custodian.
    - D. Chief financial officer.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: Each registered branch must have a person responsible for compliance, usually called the branch compliance officer (BCO) or branch manager, who supervises the representatives' conduct.
    </details>

7. Before becoming registered as a dealing representative, an individual may NOT:
    - A. Receive a redemption request for processing.
    - B. Refer a client to a registered representative.
    - C. Provide a client's current NAVPS and unit balance.
    - D. Recommend a specific mutual fund.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: Unregistered staff can handle administrative tasks, pass along basic holdings information, and refer clients. Giving advice or recommendations about mutual funds requires registration.
    </details>

8. If a dealing representative's registration isn't reactivated with a new dealer within the permitted period (generally six months), the registration will:
    - A. Remain active indefinitely.
    - B. Lapse, requiring reapplication.
    - C. Transfer automatically to the AMF.
    - D. Convert to an inactive licence for five years.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: Registration is suspended as soon as the representative leaves a dealer. If a new dealer doesn't sponsor reactivation within about six months, it lapses and the person must reapply.
    </details>

9. A dealing representative wants to also work as a life insurance agent. Among the conditions for this dual employment, the representative must:
    - A. Keep the other occupation confidential from the dealer.
    - B. Avoid disclosing the arrangement to clients.
    - C. Obtain the dealer's written approval beforehand.
    - D. Notify the MFDA only after starting.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Outside activities need the dealer's prior written approval, must not bring the industry into disrepute, and must be clearly disclosed to clients as not being the dealer's business.
    </details>

10. KYC and suitability are two of the three pillars of registrant expectations. The third pillar is:
    - A. Know Your Product.
    - B. Anti-money laundering compliance.
    - C. Privacy law compliance.
    - D. Relationship disclosure.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: KYC, KYP, and suitability are the three interdependent "golden rules." Relationship disclosure is also part of the CFRs, but it isn't one of the three pillars.
    </details>

11. Which TWO KYC requirements were introduced or clarified under the Client Focused Reforms? (Choose TWO)
    - A. KYC is collected once at account opening, with no further review.
    - B. KYC must be reviewed with the client at least every 36 months.
    - C. Clients may not update their own KYC information.
    - D. Reps must take reasonable steps to have clients confirm their KYC.
    - E. KYC updates are required only when a client complains.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B, D
      Explanation: The CFRs require reasonable steps to get the client's confirmation that their KYC is accurate, updates when a significant change comes to light, and a review at least every 36 months even without contact.
    </details>

12. A client has high risk tolerance (willingness) but low risk capacity (ability to absorb losses). Under NI 31-103 guidance, the client's risk profile should reflect:
    - A. The higher of the two.
    - B. An average of the two.
    - C. The lower of the two.
    - D. Risk tolerance only.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: The risk profile takes into account both willingness and ability, and must reflect whichever is lower.
    </details>

13. A representative receives an unsolicited order that is unsuitable given the client's KYC profile. Simply marking the order "unsolicited" on the file is:
    - A. Not sufficient; appropriate measures are required.
    - B. Enough to satisfy the representative's obligations.
    - C. Enough to remove any need to review suitability.
    - D. Required for every trade the client initiates.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: The representative must tell the client the trade isn't suitable, recommend a suitable alternative, and document the client's instructions to proceed. In some cases it may be wiser to refuse the trade.
    </details>

14. For an investment club to qualify as an exempt "private mutual fund" under legislation such as the British Columbia Securities Act, it must meet criteria including:
    - A. More than 500 members.
    - B. A paid manager chosen from its members.
    - C. A history of issuing public debt.
    - D. No more than 50 members contributing pro rata.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: The club must have no more than 50 shareholders, never have issued public debt, not pay members for advice, and have all members contribute pro rata.
    </details>

15. An account registered directly in the owner's name with the mutual fund company, rather than in the dealer's name, is called a:
    - A. Nominee account.
    - B. Client name account.
    - C. Discretionary account.
    - D. Joint account.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: A client name account is registered in the owner's own name with the fund. A nominee account is registered in the dealer's name on the client's behalf.
    </details>

16. Before any transaction under a referral arrangement, MFDA rules require the client to receive written disclosure that includes:
    - A. Nothing; no disclosure is required.
    - B. Only the referring representative's name.
    - C. How the fee is calculated and who pays and receives it.
    - D. The client's own KYC information.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: Referral arrangements need a written agreement, must be recorded on the dealer's books, and must be disclosed to the client in writing beforehand, including how the fee is calculated and who pays and receives it.
    </details>

17. A representative tells a client the exact NAVPS their order will receive before the daily valuation, then backdates an order to get the previous day's price. This is:
    - A. A prohibited selling practice.
    - B. A permitted courtesy to valued clients.
    - C. Required under NI 81-102.
    - D. Prohibited only if the client objects.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: Orders are filled at the next NAVPS calculated after the order is received (forward pricing). Quoting a future price or backdating an order to get an earlier price is prohibited.
    </details>

18. A representative offers to personally buy back a client's units if their value falls, to discourage the client from redeeming during a downturn. This is:
    - A. An acceptable way to retain assets.
    - B. Required disclosure in Fund Facts.
    - C. Prohibited only in Quebec.
    - D. A prohibited selling practice.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: Offering to repurchase securities to protect investors from price declines is prohibited. Clients already have the normal right of redemption.
    </details>

19. A representative promises a client that a fund's NAVPS will reach a specific value within a year. This is:
    - A. Acceptable if said only verbally.
    - B. Prohibited.
    - C. Required in sales communications.
    - D. Prohibited only for equity funds.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: Representatives may not promise that a fund's NAVPS will reach a certain level or rise by any amount, whether verbally or in writing.
    </details>

20. A representative accepts a general power of attorney from a client and uses it to decide when and at what price to trade in the client's account without consulting them. This is:
    - A. A permitted limited trading authorization.
    - B. A standard referral arrangement.
    - C. Prohibited discretionary trading.
    - D. Required KYP practice.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: MFDA representatives may not accept a general power of attorney to trade at their own discretion. A limited trading authorization, approved and documented, is a narrow exception for executing the client's instructions.
    </details>

21. Under National Instrument 81-102, before a mutual fund sales communication (such as an advertisement) is sent out, it requires:
    - A. Prior approval and consistency with Fund Facts.
    - B. No review of any kind.
    - C. Approval only from the client receiving it.
    - D. Verbal sign-off from a colleague.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: Sales communications must be approved before use, must not be misleading, and must be consistent with the fund's Fund Facts and simplified prospectus.
    </details>

22. Under MFDA Policy No. 3, a written client complaint must be:
    - A. Ignored unless over a dollar threshold.
    - B. Resolved without written acknowledgment.
    - C. Handled only by the representative involved.
    - D. Acknowledged and answered in writing.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: Written complaints must be acknowledged in writing, investigated by a qualified supervisor or compliance staff, and the results sent to the client in writing.
    </details>

23. Which TWO of the following are obligations imposed on firms under the Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA)? (Choose TWO)
    - A. Refuse service if a client declines non-essential collection.
    - B. Obtain consent to collect, use, or disclose personal information.
    - C. Exempt information collected electronically.
    - D. Collect information by any means necessary.
    - E. Keep privacy policies clear and readily available.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B, E
      Explanation: PIPEDA requires consent, fair and lawful collection, and clear, readily available privacy policies. Firms must still provide service if a client refuses consent for information that isn't essential to the transaction.
    </details>

24. The federal Privacy Commissioner, established under PIPEDA, has the power to:
    - A. Set mutual fund fees.
    - B. License dealing representatives.
    - C. Investigate complaints and audit practices.
    - D. Approve fund prospectuses.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: The Privacy Commissioner receives and investigates complaints, tries to resolve them, and can audit an organization's handling of personal information. Unresolved cases can go to Federal Court.
    </details>

25. Money laundering is best defined as:
    - A. Making criminal proceeds appear legitimate.
    - B. Any cash transaction over $10,000.
    - C. A legal method of reducing tax.
    - D. Moving money offshore for any reason.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: A
      Explanation: Money laundering hides the illegal source of funds so they appear clean. It's a Criminal Code offence. Large cash transactions must be reported, but they aren't laundering in themselves.
    </details>

26. According to the textbook's diagram, the three basic stages of money laundering are:
    - A. Registration, disclosure, and enforcement.
    - B. Reporting, investigation, and prosecution.
    - C. Deposit, transfer, and withdrawal.
    - D. Placement, layering, and integration.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: Placement puts illicit cash into the financial system, layering hides its trail through complex transactions, and integration returns it to the economy looking legitimate.
    </details>

27. Under anti-money laundering legislation, reporting entities such as securities dealers must report cash deposits of:
    - A. Any amount.
    - B. $10,000 or more.
    - C. $1,000 or more.
    - D. $25,000 or more.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: Cash transactions of $10,000 or more must be reported. Suspicious transactions must be reported whatever the amount.
    </details>

28. Suspicious or prescribed transactions identified by a mutual fund dealer are ultimately reported, through the dealer's compliance unit, to:
    - A. The Bank of Canada.
    - B. The Privacy Commissioner.
    - C. FINTRAC.
    - D. The FATF.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: C
      Explanation: The Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada (FINTRAC) receives these reports. The FATF is an international policy body and doesn't collect reports from dealers.
    </details>

29. The inter-governmental body that develops and promotes policies to combat money laundering and terrorist financing internationally is the:
    - A. MFDA.
    - B. CSA.
    - C. AMF.
    - D. FATF.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: D
      Explanation: The Financial Action Task Force sets international standards, monitors member countries' progress, and publishes a list of non-cooperating countries.
    </details>

30. Regulatory initiatives encourage representatives to add which of the following to a vulnerable client's file, to be contacted in circumstances such as suspected diminished capacity?
    - A. A power of attorney for the representative.
    - B. A trusted contact person.
    - C. A referral disclosure form.
    - D. A discretionary trading authorization.

    <details markdown=1><summary markdown='span'>Answer</summary>
      Correct answer: B
      Explanation: A trusted contact person (TCP) can be contacted if the representative suspects financial abuse or diminished capacity. Accepting a power of attorney or trading discretion would itself be a problem.
    </details>
